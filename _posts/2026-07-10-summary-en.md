## [Thermodynamic theory reveals why temperature matters in solid-state reactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06685) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A new paper presents a thermodynamic theory that explains the critical onset temperature in solid-state reactions through the formation of a transient liquid phase above the metastable eutectic temperature. This non-equilibrium liquid acts as a fast diffusion medium to intermix precursors and initiate reactions. This theory provides the first mechanistic framework to predict optimal synthesis temperatures for solid-state reactions, which are widely used in manufacturing ceramics, alloys, and battery materials. It could enable more efficient and controlled materials synthesis with significant industrial impact. The principle is agnostic to the structure or chemistry of reactants, meaning it should apply broadly. Recent in situ experiments had shown that solid-state reactions can complete in minutes above a critical temperature, contradicting the traditional view of slow diffusion-limited processes.

**Background**: Solid-state synthesis traditionally assumes reactions are diffusion-limited and slow. However, recent experiments revealed rapid completion above a certain temperature. The newly proposed theory attributes this to transient liquid phase formation at metastable eutectic temperatures, where a liquid temporarily appears and enhances diffusion.

## [Coaxial Hall effect enables deterministic Néel order switching in altermagnets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06730) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers discovered a coaxial Hall effect in altermagnets where induced spin and orbital magnetizations align parallel to the Néel vector, enabling deterministic switching of the Néel order under weak external fields. This was validated using oriented spin group theory and first-principles calculations on KV2Se2O. This work provides a systematic symmetry approach to identify altermagnetic candidates with large anomalous Hall effect and minimal net magnetization, paving the way for stray-field-free spintronic applications. Deterministic switching of Néel order is a major advance for antiferromagnetic spintronics. The coaxial Hall effect arises only when opposite-spin sublattices are connected through a fourfold rotation, causing orbital and spin magnetizations to have distinct perturbative orders under spin-orbit coupling. The study used oriented spin group theory and spin-orbit-coupling tensor expansion to systematically disentangle these behaviors.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 9, 04:00

**Background**: Altermagnets are a recently discovered class of magnetic materials with collinear compensated magnetic moments but non-relativistic alternating spin splitting in their band structure, combining properties of ferromagnets and antiferromagnets. The anomalous Hall effect is typically associated with ferromagnets, but altermagnets can exhibit it despite zero net magnetization due to spin-orbit coupling and crystal symmetry. The Néel vector describes the direction of staggered magnetization in antiferromagnets, and its deterministic switching is crucial for spintronic memory and logic devices.

## [Moiré Phonon Condensation Discovery in Twisted Bilayer Graphene](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06711) ⭐️ 9.0/10

This paper identifies a new mechanism called Moiré Phonon Condensation (MPC) that explains the structural crossover from breathing corrugation to common bending near the magic angle in twisted bilayer graphene. This work provides a fundamental understanding of the structural phase transition in magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene, advancing the field of moiré materials and potentially influencing the design of twistronic devices. At θ=1.08°, the displacements of all 11,164 atoms in the moiré supercell are captured by only two A₁ phonon modes at over 99.5% spectral weight, showing extreme compression. The mechanism involves soft-mode condensation where flexural stiffness changes sign near the magic angle.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 9, 04:00

**Background**: Twisted bilayer graphene consists of two graphene layers rotated relative to each other by a small angle, creating a moiré pattern. At the magic angle (~1.1°), the system exhibits strongly correlated electronic phases. This paper reveals that the structural reconstruction of the layers is driven by condensation of A₁ moiré flexural phonons.

## [Grain boundaries exhibit inertia, introducing effective interface mass](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02372) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers demonstrate that grain boundaries exhibit measurable inertial behavior under high-frequency oscillatory driving, challenging the long-held assumption of massless interfaces. They introduce a method to extract effective interface mass from the phase lag between applied force and interface velocity. This finding could fundamentally alter the understanding of microstructural evolution by establishing effective interface mass as a key parameter in interface migration. It has implications for materials processing and thermal fluctuation effects at high frequencies. The effective mass scales with the number of atoms participating in boundary migration, and inertial effects become significant at frequencies relevant to thermal fluctuations. The study uses a phase-lag method to quantify the mass, which is a novel approach in interface kinetics.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 9, 04:00

**Background**: Interfaces, such as grain boundaries, are traditionally treated as massless objects with overdamped dynamics, meaning their motion is dominated by viscous forces and inertia is neglected. This assumption has been foundational in theories of microstructural evolution for over a century. The new work challenges this by showing that under high-frequency driving, inertial effects become measurable, requiring a revised theoretical framework.

## [Critical SO(5) scaling observed in honeycomb lattice entanglement entropy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07248) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers computed Rényi entanglement entropy for various bipartitions in a honeycomb lattice model at a deconfined quantum critical point (DQCP) and found scaling consistent with an emergent SO(5) conformal field theory for corners, but observed a puzzling period-three oscillation for the hexagon subsystem. This study addresses conflicting entanglement scaling results from earlier square-lattice DQCP studies and extends the test of conformal field theory predictions to honeycomb lattice symmetry, providing crucial evidence for the nature of deconfined quantum criticality. Using large-scale quantum Monte Carlo simulations, the team found no logarithmic corrections for smooth bipartitions, ruling out Goldstone modes, while corner contributions for 60° and 120° angles closely matched SO(5) CFT predictions. The hexagon subsystem exhibited subtle period-three oscillations causing sign changes in the logarithmic term.

rss · arXiv Strongly Correlated Electrons · Jul 9, 04:00

**Background**: Deconfined quantum critical points (DQCPs) describe continuous phase transitions between ordered phases beyond Landau-Ginzburg-Wilson theory, featuring emergent gauge fields and fractionalized excitations. Entanglement entropy scaling at quantum critical points can reveal conformal field theory properties, but earlier square-lattice studies showed conflicting results. This work tests DQCP scaling on a honeycomb lattice with different crystalline symmetry.

## [Degenerate coupled-cluster theory: a black-box ansatz for multireference systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17163) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A new size-extensive, convergent, black-box coupled-cluster ansatz (ΔCC) is introduced, capable of handling degenerate and nondegenerate references, and a quasidegenerate coupled-cluster (QCC) theory is also developed for strong correlation. This addresses a long-standing challenge in quantum chemistry for accurately describing strongly correlated materials and degenerate systems. The method's black-box nature and convergence properties make it broadly applicable for electronic structure calculations. The implemented algorithms achieve up to octuple excitations and show superior performance compared to CI and EOM-CC.

The anomalous Hall effect is a hallmark of broken time-reversal symmetry, and here it emerges from PT breaking under pressure.

## [Magnetic Fluctuations Enhance Spin-Orbit Torque Near Curie Temperature](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06703) ⭐️ 8.0/10

This theoretical study demonstrates that magnetic fluctuations near the Curie temperature of ultrathin ferromagnets can significantly enhance the damping-like spin-orbit torque while suppressing the field-like torque. The mechanism involves fluctuation-driven mixing of longitudinal and transverse interfacial spin conductances, enabling enhanced absorption of transverse spin currents. This discovery provides a new physical mechanism for controlling spin currents in spintronic devices, potentially enabling more energy-efficient magnetization switching. It suggests a spintronic analog of heat-assisted magnetic recording, where thermal fluctuations are harnessed rather than being a nuisance. The study focuses on ultrathin-film magnetic heterostructures where the Curie temperature is suppressed by confinement. Above TC, the damping-like spin-orbit field is enhanced while the field-like contribution is suppressed, both showing opposite field dependencies. The effect can be activated below TC by engineering the magnetic state or using spin-current-generated short-wavelength magnons.

rss · arXiv Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics · Jul 9, 04:00

**Background**: Spin-orbit torque (SOT) is a phenomenon where a charge current in a heavy-metal layer generates a spin current via the spin Hall effect, exerting torque on an adjacent ferromagnet. Damping-like torque and field-like torque are two components of SOT that affect magnetization dynamics differently. The Curie temperature (TC) is the temperature above which ferromagnetic order is lost. Interfacial spin conductance describes how efficiently spin current is transmitted across an interface; spin-mixing conductance is a key parameter. Magnons are quantized spin waves that can carry spin current.
