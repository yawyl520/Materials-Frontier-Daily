## [100-second quantum coherence in SiC spin defects via electrical depletion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13164) ⭐️ 10.0/10

Researchers demonstrated over 100-second Hahn echo coherence times in silicon carbide spin defects by electrically depleting magnetic noise using a p-i-n diode. This is the longest reported Hahn echo time for any single spin platform. This breakthrough extends quantum coherence times by orders of magnitude, enabling practical quantum information processing in solid-state devices. It leverages existing semiconductor manufacturing, potentially accelerating integration of quantum technologies with classical electronics. The experiment used an isotopically purified 4H-SiC p-i-n diode to suppress both electrical and magnetic noise sources. Hahn echo times exceeded 100 seconds, and relaxation times were also significantly extended.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 15, 04:00

**Background**: Quantum coherence is the ability of a quantum system to maintain a superposition state, essential for quantum computing. Spin defects in solids, like nitrogen-vacancy centers in diamond, are promising qubits but limited by magnetic noise from surrounding nuclear spins. The Hahn echo sequence refocuses spin magnetization to measure coherence time (T2). A p-i-n diode allows electrical control to deplete charge carriers that cause magnetic fluctuations. Isotopic purification removes isotopes with nuclear spins that contribute to noise, further extending coherence.

## [Emergent toroidal induction in a polar Weyl ferromagnet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12322) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers demonstrate emergent electromagnetic induction (EEMI) in a spatially uniform ferromagnet via a novel Berry-phase mechanism, using spin-orbit torques in the polar Weyl ferromagnet PrAlGe to generate a toroidal moment that induces a Hall voltage. This discovery expands spintronics to uniform ferromagnets without requiring magnetic textures, offering a new pathway for energy-efficient spin-charge interconversion and unifying Berry phase, topology, and spin dynamics. The effect manifests as an out-of-phase AC Hall response that scales linearly with frequency, with first-principles calculations showing that the toroidal vector encodes the collective motion of Weyl nodes in momentum space.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 15, 04:00

**Background**: Emergent electromagnetic induction (EEMI) traditionally requires magnetic textures like domain walls or skyrmions for a Berry-phase-driven electromotive force. ## [Quantized Photocurrents Observed in Chiral Topological Semimetal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12420) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers directly observed a quantized circular photogalvanic effect in the chiral topological semimetal Rh0.95Ni0.05Si via 5% Ni substitution, which opens a photon-energy window dominated by interband transitions at a multifold node. The effect exhibits three hallmarks of quantization: a sharp onset, a wavelength-independent plateau proportional to the monopole charge, and a Pauli-blocking cutoff. This work realizes an all-optical analogue of the quantum Hall effect in gapless matter, resolving a long-standing challenge in topological quantum materials. It establishes a new paradigm for topological quantization and has potential implications for quantum photovoltaics and optoelectronics. The circular photogalvanic effect was measured with circularly polarized near- to mid-infrared pulses generating a helicity-odd terahertz response. The quantization plateau emerges only when optical transitions are confined to a single multifold node, and the cutoff arises from Pauli blocking of interband transitions.

rss · arXiv Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics · Jul 15, 04:00

**Background**: The quantum Hall effect is a hallmark of topologically nontrivial gapped systems, but realizing similar quantization in gapless materials has been challenging. Chiral topological semimetals lack inversion symmetry and host multifold band crossings that act as Berry-curvature monopoles. The circular photogalvanic effect (CPGE) is a nonlinear optical current that reverses with light helicity; its quantization is predicted when only one node contributes. However, trivial bands and multiple nodes typically obscure the effect.

The optical nonlinear anomalous Hall effect arises from the quantum metric of Bloch bands, providing a new probe of Néel order.

This advance provides a direct way to extract topological invariants from expectation values in 2D, facilitating experimental and numerical detection of SPT phases and deepening the understanding of multipartite entanglement in topological matter. Multi-entropy is a replica-based generalization of entanglement entropy that quantifies genuine multipartite entanglement.

## [Mathematical Analysis of Martensite Compatibility in Polycrystals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12572) ⭐️ 8.0/10

This paper rigorously analyzes martensitic microstructure compatibility across grain boundaries in polycrystals, proving that for cubic-to-tetragonal transformations, compatibility is only possible for a set of measure zero, which explains the prevalence of higher-order laminates. These findings provide a fundamental theoretical understanding of microstructure evolution in shape-memory alloys and related materials, guiding the design of polycrystalline materials with optimized properties. The study uses computer-assisted symbolic calculations to show that compatibility of simple laminates across grain boundaries is only possible for a closed set of measure zero in the manifold of grain boundary normals and relative rotations. A similar but slightly weaker result holds for cubic-to-orthorhombic transformations.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 15, 04:00

**Background**: Martensitic transformations involve a structural change between a high-temperature austenite phase and a low-temperature martensite phase, often producing complex microstructures like laminates to minimize elastic energy. In polycrystals, grain boundaries impose geometric constraints that affect microstructure compatibility, but a full mathematical understanding has been lacking. This work provides rigorous proofs for the compatibility limitations, explaining why higher-order laminates are frequently observed.
