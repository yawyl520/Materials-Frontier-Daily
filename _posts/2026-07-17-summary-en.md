## [First Bulk Synthesis of Elusive Iodide Double Perovskite Cs2AgBiI6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13180) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers report the first bulk synthesis of the iodide double-perovskite Cs2AgBiI6 via anion exchange from Cs2AgBiBr6 microcrystals, yielding phase-pure microcrystals and thin films with a 1.70 eV bandgap and near-infrared photoluminescence. This breakthrough finally realizes a computationally predicted but synthetically elusive lead-free double perovskite, opening new possibilities for stable, lead-free photovoltaics and optoelectronics. The key to success was eliminating trace moisture in bulk Cs2AgBiBr6, enabling complete halide exchange and isolation. The resulting Cs2AgBiI6 shows stability up to ~70°C and in dry air, and photoconductivity measurements confirm photocarrier generation and transport.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 16, 04:00

**Background**: Three-dimensional iodide double perovskites (elpasolites) such as Cs2AgBiI6 are of interest as lead-free alternatives to lead-halide perovskites in solar cells. However, they have been predicted to be thermodynamically unstable and have remained experimentally inaccessible until now. Anion exchange is a method where halide ions in a perovskite are replaced by another halide, but achieving complete exchange without decomposition has been challenging.

## [Direct Imaging Reveals Percolation of Polar Nanoregions in PMN Relaxor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13364) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers have directly imaged the temperature evolution of polar nanoregions (PNRs) in the prototypical relaxor ferroelectric Pb(Mg1/3Nb2/3)O3 using convergent-beam electron diffraction and 4D-STEM, revealing a percolation transition from isolated PNRs at room temperature to interconnected structures at low temperatures. This provides the first direct real-space evidence for the percolation model of polar nanoregions, a long-standing challenge in condensed matter physics. The findings have implications for understanding relaxor properties and designing high-energy storage dielectric materials. The study maps both PNRs and chemically ordered regions (CORs), finding that static CORs (2-5 nm) suppress PNR growth, while PNRs evolve from 2-5 nm isolated regions to ~10 nm interconnected clusters upon cooling, indicative of a percolation transition. The observations support the random-field model.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 16, 04:00

**Background**: Relaxor ferroelectrics, such as PMN, exhibit exceptional dielectric and piezoelectric properties due to polar nanoregions that form below the Burns temperature. The random-field model posits that these PNRs emerge from a paraelectric matrix and are constrained by random local fields from chemical disorder. Direct real-space visualization of PNR behavior has been a major experimental challenge. This study uses advanced electron microscopy techniques to overcome this.

## [3D Non-Foliated FQH Phases with Irrational Anyons in Twisted van der Waals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13127) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers theoretically propose that large-angle twisted van der Waals multilayers can host three-dimensional non-foliated fractional quantum Hall phases featuring quasiparticles with irrational braiding statistics, supported by Monte Carlo calculations. This work provides a realistic materials platform for intrinsically three-dimensional topological order beyond stacked 2D systems, potentially enabling new quantum information storage and processing resources. The study used Monte Carlo comparisons of 862 competing trial wavefunctions and found that generalized Halperin states with quantum coherence across multiple layers are stabilized. The resulting phases host quasiparticles with rational electric charge but irrational braiding statistics, closely related to fractonic topological order.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 16, 04:00

**Background**: The fractional quantum Hall effect (FQHE) occurs in 2D electron gases under strong magnetic fields, exhibiting anyonic quasiparticles with fractional charge. Three-dimensional FQH phases have been sought for higher-dimensional topological order but are difficult to realize because Landau quantization in 3D typically produces dispersive 1D bands favoring metallic states. Twisted van der Waals multilayers with large twist angles suppress interlayer tunneling while preserving strong Coulomb interactions, offering a pathway to stabilize 3D FQH states.

## [Precision quantum simulation of magnon spectra and interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13301) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers used a 97-qubit superconducting processor to precisely simulate magnon spectra and interactions in a 2D XY spin-1/2 magnet, combining analog-digital techniques with Hamiltonian learning. This work demonstrates high-precision quantum simulation of interacting quasiparticles in quantum magnets, offering new capabilities for studying magnetic excitations and potentially accelerating materials discovery in condensed matter physics. The simulation measured linear magnon response (temperature-dependent spectra and lifetimes) and nonlinear effects (self-scattering and pump-probe spectroscopy) across a 97-qubit system. Results showed magnon decay rates vary with Brillouin zone location, enhanced near van Hove singularities, and were compared to matrix-product state simulations, which become inaccurate for larger systems or higher temperatures.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 16, 04:00

**Background**: Magnons are collective spin-wave excitations in magnetic materials, fundamental to understanding magnetism and spin dynamics. Quantum simulation uses controllable quantum systems to study complex quantum phenomena; superconducting processors with many qubits enable such simulations. This work employs an analog-digital approach that interleaves digital gates with analog evolution, and uses Hamiltonian learning to precisely characterize the system's dynamics.

## [First topological skyrmions created in thermal light](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13542) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers have demonstrated for the first time that thermal light can be engineered to carry optical topologies in the form of Skyrmions, using a metasurface to imprint spatial structure that remains immune to time-averaged decoherence. This was validated through both experimental measurements and simulations. This work breaks the previous requirement for coherent light sources to create optical topologies, opening the door to exploiting topological phenomena with ubiquitous, low-cost thermal sources. It could enable new applications in topological photonics, imaging, and information processing using everyday light. The topology is realized as Skyrmions, a type of quasiparticle with a twisted spin texture, which are inherently robust against decoherence and also against material and fabrication defects in the metasurface generation stage. Simulations indicate that further coherence management in the metasurface design could enhance functionality.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 16, 04:00

**Background**: Optical skyrmions are topologically protected spin textures in light fields, previously generated only using coherent laser sources because the spatial phase structure must be preserved. Thermal light, such as from an incandescent bulb, is spatially and temporally incoherent, which normally washes out any imposed phase patterns. This work overcomes that limitation by exploiting the topology's immunity to decoherence.

## [Dislocation-ledge coupling governs semicoherent precipitate growth](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16158) ⭐️ 9.0/10

This study reveals that semicoherent precipitate growth is governed by dislocation-ledge coupling, where interfacial dislocation networks advance via diffusion-assisted non-conservative motion, resolving a long-standing puzzle in phase transformations. This work provides a general defect-kinetic route connecting point-defect transport, dislocation-network motion, interface migration, and morphology evolution, with high impact on physical metallurgy and phase transformation understanding. Using in situ TEM, O-lattice analysis, and 3D phase-field crystal simulations, the study observed nanometer-high growth ledges propagating laterally along migrating habit planes in austenite precipitates in duplex stainless steel. The simulations resolved hidden 3D dynamics showing diffusion-assisted non-conservative motion of the dislocation network.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 16, 04:00

**Background**: Semicoherent interfaces contain periodic arrays of dislocations that accommodate lattice misfit between two crystal phases. Traditionally, dislocation motion was thought to occur primarily by conservative glide, but dense interfacial networks cannot easily glide; this study reveals that non-conservative motion (climb) coupled with ledge propagation is the key mechanism.

This work demonstrates two-dimensional mapping of electron g-factors in Si/SiGe quantum dots with sub-10^-3 precision and nanometer lateral resolution using conveyor-mode shuttling of entangled spin pairs. It reveals two g-factors per quantum dot site related to valley states and extracts complex intervalley coupling parameters. This technique provides a powerful method for screening qubit material parameters in large-scale silicon spin qubit chips. It enables unprecedented insights into spin-valley dynamics during qubit manipulation, readout, and shuttling, serving as a benchmark for engineering Si/SiGe heterostructures. The experiment scans the g-factor across a 40 nm × 400 nm area and observes a bimodal distribution with striking symmetry. The g-factor maps are compared with valley splitting maps on the same device to extract intervalley coupling parameters along shuttle trajectories using a theoretical model.

**Background**: Silicon spin qubits are a leading platform for quantum computing due to long coherence times and compatibility with semiconductor manufacturing. The electron g-factor and intervalley coupling are critical material parameters that affect qubit performance. Conveyor-mode shuttling is a technique that moves electrons adiabatically in moving quantum dots, enabling high-fidelity spin transport over microns. This work combines shuttling with spin manipulation to map these parameters.

Researchers have demonstrated high-performance SiMOS quantum-dot spin qubits fabricated using extreme-ultraviolet (EUV) lithography in a 300 mm pilot line, achieving wafer-scale uniformity and high gate fidelities up to 99.9% for single-qubit gates and 99.1% for two-qubit gates. Gate set tomography (GST) is a self-consistent method for characterizing quantum operations without calibration errors.

## [99.999% Fidelity Parallel Gates in Silicon Spin Qubit Array](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11918) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers achieved record single-qubit gate fidelities above 99.99% (up to 99.999%) in a five-qubit silicon spin array during simultaneous operation, using a scalable AC Stark shift mitigation protocol based on pairwise phase calibrations. This breakthrough overcomes a central challenge in silicon spin qubits—fidelity degradation during parallel operation—paving the way for scalable quantum processors built on industrial semiconductor manufacturing. The experiment used a five-qubit silicon quantum dot array with shared control lines. With tailored pulses, individual qubit π/2 gate fidelities reached well above 99.99%, with some approaching 99.999%. Under simultaneous three-qubit operation, fidelities remained above 99.99%; for all five qubits, they stayed at the fault-tolerant threshold of 99.9%, with further improvement possible via device engineering.

rss · arXiv Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics · Jul 16, 04:00

**Background**: Silicon spin qubits are promising for scalable quantum computing because they can be fabricated using standard semiconductor processes. A qubit gate is a basic operation that manipulates the quantum state. AC Stark shift is a frequency shift of the qubit caused by the microwave drive, which becomes problematic when multiple qubits are driven simultaneously, causing crosstalk and fidelity loss. The authors identified this shift as the main obstacle and developed a simple calibration scheme to compensate for it.
