## [Light-Programmable Superconductivity Achieved in Al-Si Heterojunction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14567) ⭐️ 10.0/10

Researchers have experimentally realized a light-programmable superconducting (LiPS) state in an aluminum-silicon heterojunction, where femtosecond laser pulses create and control a true zero-resistance state with critical temperatures ranging from 1.8 to 8.5 K. This breakthrough demonstrates for the first time that macroscopic quantum phase coherence can be both created and controlled by light, a long-sought goal that could enable light-engineering of quantum circuits and switchable superconductivity devices. The LiPS state shows signatures of a Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless topological transition at low temperatures and quantum phase disorder above 2 K, with vortex pinning behavior in magnetic fields. The effect originates from light-controlled Moiré-like superlattice of misfit dislocations at the Al-Si interface.

rss · arXiv Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics · Jul 17, 04:00

**Background**: Superconductivity is a state where a material conducts electricity with zero resistance, requiring macroscopic quantum phase coherence. Heterojunctions are interfaces between dissimilar materials with different band gaps. The Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT) transition describes vortex unbinding in two-dimensional systems. Prior attempts to create light-induced superconductivity lacked direct evidence of zero resistance and full optical control.

## [Driven-dissipative superconductivity in moiré heterostructures without attraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15169) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers propose a driven-dissipative protocol to prepare superconductivity as a stationary state in a bilayer moiré heterostructure, using the layer degree of freedom as a pseudospin and optically induced spatial operations, without requiring attractive interactions. This work introduces a paradigm-shifting approach to superconductivity via driven-dissipative preparation, opening possibilities for realizing superconductivity in systems where attractive interactions are weak or absent. It also reveals a connection between steady-state pairing and superradiance, linking two seemingly distinct phenomena. The protocol relies on local dissipation naturally provided by weakly dispersive bosonic modes in the heterostructure. In contrast, under collective dissipation the same system shows an early-time superradiant burst, linking superconductivity preparation to transient superradiance.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 17, 04:00

**Background**: Moiré heterostructures are formed by stacking two-dimensional materials with a slight twist, creating a periodic superlattice that can host strongly correlated phases. Driven-dissipative systems are open systems where external drives and engineered dissipation can stabilize desired quantum states. Pseudospin refers to an internal degree of freedom, here the layer index, that mimics electron spin. Superradiance is a collective emission phenomenon where many emitters radiate coherently, producing a burst of light.

## [Coulomb blockade in metallic grains: new decoherence source in quantum circuits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15252) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers identified Coulomb blockade and microwave-driven charge tunneling in ubiquitous metallic grains as a previously unrecognized decoherence mechanism in solid-state quantum circuits, using scanning gate microscopy on live superconducting circuits. This challenges the prevailing paradigm that coherence lifetimes are primarily limited by two-level system defects, and offers a clear mitigation route by eliminating metallic grains during fabrication. The defects are as common and debilitating as two-level system defects but arise from a fundamentally different physical mechanism; conventional characterization techniques would misattribute this loss to power-independent processes.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 17, 04:00

**Background**: Coulomb blockade is a phenomenon in nanoscale devices where electron transport is suppressed at low bias due to electrostatic repulsion, commonly observed in quantum dots and small tunnel junctions. Scanning gate microscopy uses a conductive AFM tip as a movable gate to locally perturb the sample's electrostatic potential, enabling nanoscale imaging of electron transport. Decoherence in solid-state quantum circuits, such as superconducting qubits, is often attributed to two-level system defects in amorphous materials, but this work reveals an additional source from metallic grains left by lithography.

## [Quantized Photocurrents Observed in Gapless Topological Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12420) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers observed a quantized circular photogalvanic effect in the chiral topological semimetal Rh0.95Ni0.05Si, achieving a long-sought quantized photocurrent in gapless topological matter. This breakthrough establishes an all-optical analogue of the quantum Hall effect and provides a new paradigm for topological quantization in gapless matter, addressing a central challenge in quantum materials. Nickel substitution creates a photon-energy window where interband transitions at the Γ-point multifold node dominate, enabling circularly polarized near- to mid-infrared pulses to generate a helicity-odd terahertz response with three quantization hallmarks: sharp onset, a wavelength-independent plateau determined by monopole charge, and an abrupt cutoff from Pauli blocking.

rss · arXiv Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics · Jul 17, 04:00

**Background**: Chiral topological semimetals have symmetry-protected multifold band crossings that act as Berry-curvature monopoles. When optical transitions are confined to a single node, the circular photogalvanic effect (CPGE) is predicted to be quantized in terms of topological charge. However, trivial band transitions and Fermi level alignment have made this quantization experimentally elusive. The present work uses chemical substitution to isolate the relevant node, overcoming these challenges.

## [Fluidic Hysterons Enable Memory in Flow Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15122) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers have demonstrated a fluidic hysteron—a bistable unit with memory—using an elastic fiber in a microfluidic channel, driven by nonlinear elastohydrodynamic feedback. This work establishes a passive hydrodynamic route to hysteron networks where memory emerges from flow-structure interactions. This breakthrough introduces a new paradigm for memory systems based on soft matter and hydrodynamics, potentially enabling novel memory devices and insights into complex systems with hysteresis and avalanches. It bridges the gap between solid-state hysterons and fluidic systems, opening up applications in microfluidics and soft robotics. A single elastic fiber in a microfluidic channel becomes bistable through viscous loading deformation that reshapes hydraulic resistance, organized by a cusp catastrophe in geometric parameters. A parallel bypass channel can reshape or eliminate bistability, and in arrays varying a single geometric parameter transitions from a non-interacting Preisach regime with return-point memory to an interacting regime with avalanche switching and memory violation.

rss · arXiv Soft Condensed Matter · Jul 17, 04:00

**Background**: Hysterons are minimal bistable elements with distinct switching thresholds that form the basis for memory in driven matter; they have previously been realized in solid-state systems via buckling or snap-through. This work provides a hydrodynamic realization using elastohydrodynamic feedback, where fiber deformation alters flow resistance, creating a hysteretic response. Cusp catastrophe theory is a branch of dynamical systems that describes sudden changes in behavior due to small changes in control parameters, and here it explains the onset of bistability.

## [Anisotropic exciton dispersion observed in 2D CrSBr](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14712) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers directly observed anisotropic exciton dispersion in multilayer CrSBr using defocus-engineered electron energy-loss spectroscopy, revealing a linear dispersion with a slope of 7.02 eV·Å along one direction, among the largest reported in low-dimensional systems. This work establishes CrSBr as a model system for investigating anisotropic exciton dynamics in low-symmetry layered semiconductors and provides insights into the role of electron–hole exchange interaction, which could impact the design of future optoelectronic devices. The exciton dispersion shows pronounced in-plane anisotropy: linear along ΓY and nearly flat along ΓX, with a slope up to 7.02 eV·Å. First-principles calculations confirm the intrinsic origin, and measurements across the magnetic phase transition (paramagnetic to A-type antiferromagnetic) reveal no change in dispersion, indicating negligible coupling between exciton propagation and magnetic order.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 17, 04:00

**Background**: Excitons are bound electron-hole pairs that mediate light-matter interactions in semiconductors. Momentum-resolved electron energy-loss spectroscopy (qEELS) allows mapping of exciton dispersion, which describes how exciton energy varies with momentum. In low-symmetry materials like CrSBr, the exciton dispersion can be highly anisotropic due to directional selection rules of the transition dipole moment and long-range electron-hole exchange interaction. CrSBr is a layered antiferromagnetic semiconductor with strong in-plane anisotropy.

## [Computing binary alloy phase diagrams with explicit entropy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14795) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new computational framework combining alchemical thermodynamic integration, Monte Carlo identity exchange, and molecular dynamics is developed to compute binary alloy phase diagrams with explicit configurational and vibrational entropy, demonstrated for Au-Cu using Atomic Cluster Expansion potentials. This method addresses a long-standing challenge by simultaneously capturing non-ideal configurational and vibrational entropy within a single atomistic framework, significantly improving the accuracy of phase diagram predictions and having broad impact on computational thermodynamics and materials design. For Au-Cu, the method lowers the order-disorder transition temperature from ~810 K to 710 K (vs. experimental 683 K) when using LDA-based ACE potentials, but the strong sensitivity to exchange-correlation functionals (LDA, PBE, r2SCAN) indicates that careful functional choice is essential for quantitative accuracy.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 17, 04:00

**Background**: Phase diagrams are essential for understanding alloy stability, but atomistic prediction is challenging because configurational entropy beyond ideal mixing and vibrational entropy are difficult to capture together. This work extends non-equilibrium thermodynamic integration to composition-dependent transformations using alchemical interpolation between elements, combined with Monte Carlo for configurational sampling and molecular dynamics for vibrational sampling. Atomic Cluster Expansion (ACE) potentials are machine-learned interatomic potentials that provide accurate and efficient energy predictions, trained here on DFT data from different exchange-correlation functionals.

## [SevenNet-Polar: Multitask Prediction of Energy, Forces, Stress, and Born Charges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14827) ⭐️ 8.0/10

SevenNet-Polar is a new equivariant graph neural network framework that enables fast and accurate multitask prediction of energy, forces, stress, and Born effective charges, achieving RMSE as low as 0.0043 e for BEC and 1.0 meV/atom for energy on materials like ZrO2, Li3PO4, and perovskites. This breakthrough makes charge-aware molecular dynamics simulations under electric fields more accessible, enabling studies of large-scale systems with up to 1.5 million atoms on multi-GPU supercomputers or ~15,000 atoms on a single consumer GPU, which is crucial for modeling realistic materials behavior. The BEC-only predictor achieves an RMSE as low as 0.0043 e despite training on high-temperature (up to 2000 K) and defect-laden data, and multitask training does not degrade BEC accuracy. Scaling analysis reveals distinct exponents for diagonal and off-diagonal BEC components, both showing less favorable scaling than energy, force, and stress errors.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 17, 04:00

**Background**: The Born effective charge tensor describes the change in polarization induced by atomic displacement and is crucial for modeling materials under electric fields, but computing it from first principles is computationally expensive. Equivariant graph neural networks (GNNs) are neural networks that respect symmetries such as rotations and translations, making them well-suited for predicting physical properties of atomic systems. SevenNet-Polar builds on the SevenNet architecture to incorporate these capabilities.

## [Reproducibility crisis in Turkevich AuNP and CVD MoS2 syntheses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14849) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A systematic review of over 2,600 articles on Turkevich gold nanoparticle synthesis and chemical vapor deposition (CVD) growth of MoS2 reveals that only a small fraction of studies properly addressed reproducibility. Critical experimental parameters were frequently underreported, and statistical analyses were rarely included. This review highlights a significant reproducibility crisis in widely used materials synthesis methods, undermining confidence in published results and calling for improved reporting standards. The findings have the potential to change research practices across nanoscience and materials chemistry. The study employed an adapted PRISMA protocol combined with a modified SPIDER framework and a Python-based text classification algorithm validated against human reviewers. Over 1,300 articles for each synthesis method were retrieved from Scopus and Web of Science and systematically screened.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 17, 04:00

**Background**: The Turkevich method, first reported in 1951, is a classic wet-chemical route for synthesizing gold nanoparticles by reducing chloroauric acid with sodium citrate. Chemical vapor deposition (CVD) is a common technique for growing thin films of MoS2, a two-dimensional semiconductor. PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) provides guidelines for transparent and complete reporting of systematic reviews.
