## [Ferrialtermagnetism realized in CrSb flakes with multi-field valve](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11222) ⭐️ 10.0/10

Researchers propose ferrialtermagnetism as a new subclass of altermagnets and experimentally realize it in three-atomic-layer CrSb (110) flakes, demonstrating a spin splitting of 344 meV and a high Néel temperature of 657 K. They further integrate CrSb with ferroelectric Sc2CO2 and a Cu spacer to construct a ferrialtermagnetic-ferroelectric valve exhibiting tunneling magnetoresistance and electroresistance of ~10^3% under equilibrium and up to ~10^4% under bias, thermal, or light fields. This work establishes ferrialtermagnetism as a fertile platform for multi-field-controlled, ultracompact, and self-powered spintronics and electronics, potentially enabling novel memory and logic devices. The combination of large magnetoresistance and electroresistance with high spin filtering efficiency (90%) and photocurrent extinction ratio (283.8) marks a significant breakthrough in spintronics. The CrSb flakes exhibit moderate uniaxial magnetic anisotropy and support magneto-optical Kerr and anomalous Hall effects. The ferrialtermagnetic-ferroelectric valve achieves negative differential resistance and photogalvanic effects, with non-equilibrium magnetoresistance and electroresistance reaching ~10^4%.

rss · arXiv Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics · Jul 14, 04:00

**Background**: Altermagnets are a class of magnetic materials that combine zero stray field of antiferromagnets with spin polarization of ferromagnets, promising for spintronics. Ferrialtermagnetism is a new subclass where symmetry-inequivalent sublattices have nonidentical Néel vectors, leading to uncompensated spin splitting. CrSb is a 2D material with three-atomic-layer thickness, and Sc2CO2 is a ferroelectric MXene used to modulate the device via polarization reversal.

## [New Paradigm for Engineering Topological Phases in 2D Materials](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10049) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A review article outlines a paradigm shift in engineering topological phases in two-dimensional materials by exploiting interfacial coupling in van der Waals heterostructures, moving beyond the traditional reliance on intrinsic spin-orbit coupling. This approach dramatically expands the materials landscape for topological insulators, enabling realization of nontrivial topology in otherwise trivial systems and offering dynamic tunability via external stimuli, which is critical for next-generation spintronic and quantum technologies. The framework leverages multiple tunable degrees of freedom including stacking configuration, twist angle, and chemical functionalization, along with external stimuli such as electric fields, strain, and light-matter coupling, to achieve controlled topological phases in van der Waals heterostructures.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 14, 04:00

**Background**: Two-dimensional topological insulators feature robust, dissipationless edge states protected by time-reversal symmetry, traditionally realized in materials with strong intrinsic spin-orbit coupling that drives band inversion. This intrinsic requirement severely limits the available materials. Van der Waals heterostructures, formed by stacking atomically thin layers, allow engineering of electronic properties through interfacial interactions and twist-angle tuning, providing a versatile platform for creating topological phases without relying solely on intrinsic material properties.

## [Ultrafast control of nonrelativistic spin splitting via THz phononics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10091) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers demonstrate ultrafast, reversible control of nonrelativistic spin splitting in antiferromagnets NiO and LaFeO3 using THz laser pulses and nonlinear phononics, achieving ~40 meV splitting in NiO and 100% enhancement in LaFeO3. This work establishes nonlinear phononics as a general route for ultrafast manipulation of spin-split antiferromagnetic phases, opening new possibilities for high-frequency spintronics and ultrafast magnetism beyond static strain methods. The study derives two symmetry criteria based on phonon-magnetic wavevector compatibility and order-parameter parity to identify Raman-active phonon modes that can activate or amplify NRSS. In NiO, resonant driving of an infrared-active mode at 11.08 THz via biquadratic anharmonic coupling transiently generates spin splitting, accompanied by a transient SOC-induced net moment detectable by magneto-optical Kerr effect.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 14, 04:00

**Background**: Nonrelativistic spin splitting (NRSS) in collinear antiferromagnets allows spin-polarized band structures without net magnetization, promising high-frequency spintronics free from stray fields. However, dynamic control of NRSS has been challenging. THz nonlinear phononics uses intense THz pulses to coherently drive lattice vibrations, enabling ultrafast manipulation of crystal symmetry and properties, often accessing nonequilibrium states.

## [Altermagnetism Catalog for All Non-Centrosymmetric Crystals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10857) ⭐️ 9.0/10

This work provides a complete catalog of altermagnetic orders for all 2D magnetic wallpaper groups and 3D magnetic space groups, including 17 wallpaper groups and 422 space groups, identifying both centrosymmetric and non-centrosymmetric cases. This comprehensive catalog extends altermagnetism to all non-centrosymmetric crystals, providing a crucial resource for condensed matter physics and spintronics research by enabling targeted material discovery. The authors use a non-spatial global symmetry, spinless time-reversal symmetry, to replace inversion symmetry, and assign each group to one of six altermagnetic wave types with distinct Brillouin zone spin distributions.

rss · arXiv Strongly Correlated Electrons · Jul 14, 04:00

**Background**: Altermagnetism is a recently identified class of magnetic order with compensated collinear spins and spin splitting, but unlike antiferromagnets, it exhibits spin splitting without net magnetization. This work simplifies classification by leveraging a modified time-reversal symmetry, enabling systematic analysis of all magnetic space groups.

## [d-Electron Heavy-Fermion Superconductor via Frustrated Lattice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09924) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers report Mo4PtGa17, a d-electron heavy-fermion-like superconductor that achieves its properties through geometrical frustration of the Mo lattice, without f-electrons. The material exhibits enhanced spin fluctuations and heavy quasiparticle mass near a ferromagnetic instability. This discovery opens a new route to heavy-fermion superconductivity in d-electron systems, challenging the traditional f-electron paradigm. It may enable the design of unconventional superconductors with enhanced critical temperatures and novel electronic phases. Mo4PtGa17 has a noncentrosymmetric breathing-pyrochlore structure with geometrically frustrated Mo sites. Thermodynamic, transport, and NMR measurements confirm heavy-fermion behavior, while band structure calculations reveal nearly flat bands and van Hove singularities from Mo-4d states.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 14, 04:00

**Background**: Heavy-fermion superconductors are typically found in f-electron materials with Kondo lattices, where local moments hybridize with conduction electrons. Geometrical frustration, arising from competing interactions on a triangular or pyrochlore lattice, can suppress magnetic order and enhance electronic correlations. This work demonstrates that frustration alone can generate heavy-fermion-like behavior in d-electron systems without f-electrons.

## [3D bulk-resolved g-wave altermagnetic order parameter in CrSb](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14526) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers have demonstrated that magnetic quantum oscillation measurements can provide a high-resolution, bulk-sensitive, 3D mapping of the altermagnetic order parameter in CrSb, revealing a g-wave symmetry analogous to a hydrogen g-orbital. This work establishes a new method for precisely mapping order parameter symmetry in unconventional magnets, which has been notoriously difficult to resolve even in unconventional superconductors. It identifies CrSb as a prototypical g-wave metallic altermagnet, advancing the understanding of altermagnetism. By rotating the magnetic field through high- and low-symmetry directions of the CrSb Brillouin zone, the exchange splitting between up and down spins follows the profile of the real spherical harmonic Y_4^{-3} = zy(3x^2-y^2). This demonstrates a g-wave symmetry, which is a higher-order angular momentum channel.

rss · arXiv Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics · Jul 14, 04:00

**Background**: Altermagnets are a class of magnetic materials where up- and down-spin electrons have non-degenerate band structures in momentum space, leading to unconventional magnetic order. Quantum oscillations are periodic variations in physical properties as a function of magnetic field, which can probe the Fermi surface and band structure. This study uses these oscillations to map the order parameter symmetry in three dimensions.

## [Electrical Spectroscopy of Intervalley Relaxation in WSe2 Transistors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10470) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers demonstrate that the transconductance of multilayer WSe2 field-effect transistors can directly measure the intervalley relaxation time, a parameter previously only accessible via ultrafast optical techniques. This work introduces an all-electrical method to probe valley dynamics in 2D materials, which could accelerate the development of valleytronics by enabling routine measurements with standard instrumentation. The method predicts three distinct electrical signatures: a Lorentzian transconductance with layer-dependent sign, a two-stage current transient after a gate step, and sweep-rate-proportional hysteresis, all quantitatively linked to the intervalley relaxation time.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 14, 04:00

**Background**: Valleytronics exploits the valley degree of freedom in semiconductors, analogous to spintronics with spin. In transition metal dichalcogenides like WSe2, carriers can occupy different valleys (K and Γ points) in the Brillouin zone. The intervalley relaxation time is a key parameter governing how long valley polarization persists, but has been challenging to measure electrically. This work uses the transconductance of field-effect transistors, which measures how the drain current changes with gate voltage, as a sensitive probe of the valley population dynamics.

**Tags**: `#valleytronics`, `#WSe2`, `#intervalley relaxation`, `#2D materials`, `#transconductance`

## [Dual Spectral-Domain Deep Learning for 3D Particle Scattering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10570) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers introduced a dual spectral-domain neural scattering model that compresses particle geometry into 256 spherical-harmonic coefficients and predicts the T-matrix via a spectral-token Transformer, enabling rapid scattering prediction for arbitrarily shaped 3D particles. This approach addresses the challenge of large morphological variability and strong angular dependence in particle optics, offering a compact and reusable method for angle- and polarization-resolved optical scattering prediction, which could accelerate photonic characterization and inverse design. The model uses spherical harmonics to represent morphology as a compact ordered descriptor, and the T-matrix encodes the optical response in a spherical-vector-wave basis. A Transformer trained on 50,000 irregular particles at 1064 nm maps the morphology spectrum directly to the T-matrix, generalizing to out-of-distribution shapes and natural sand particles.

rss · arXiv Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics · Jul 14, 04:00

**Background**: Optical scattering from 3D particles is important for applications like particle characterization and remote sensing, but traditional methods (e.g., Mie theory) are limited to simple shapes. Spherical harmonics provide a compact representation of arbitrary 3D geometries, while the T-matrix is a fundamental operator that fully describes the scattering response of a particle in a spherical-wave basis. Combining these with deep learning enables efficient prediction without iterative calculations.

## [Purcell enhanced single photons from InAs/GaAs quantum dots in circular Bragg gratings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11495) ⭐️ 9.0/10

This paper demonstrates a bright, charge-tunable single-photon source from InAs quantum dots embedded in a modified circular Bragg grating, achieving blinking-free emission with a Purcell factor of 18.0 and extraction efficiency of 68.1%. This advancement directly addresses key requirements for quantum communication and photonic quantum computing, offering deterministic charge control and high efficiency simultaneously. The device uses a p-i-n doped GaAs membrane with a labyrinth-like bridge design to enable electrical contact while preserving optical performance. The negative trion state shows a lifetime of 44.3 ps, a Purcell factor of 18.0, and a second-order autocorrelation g^(2)(0) < 0.017, indicating very low multi-photon emission.

**Background**: Single-photon sources are essential for quantum technologies. InAs/GaAs quantum dots are promising but often suffer from blinking and low efficiency. Circular Bragg gratings can enhance light-matter interaction via Purcell effect, but previous designs limited electrical tunability. ## [Symmetry-driven electronic cascade in d-wave altermagnet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11567) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers discovered a symmetry-driven cascade of charge orders in the d-wave altermagnet Rb1-δV2Te2O using phase-resolved scanning tunneling microscopy, revealing a hierarchical electronic reconstruction with multiple density-wave instabilities. This discovery demonstrates that altermagnetic symmetry can organize collective electronic order beyond band formation, offering a new paradigm for understanding emergent many-body states in correlated quantum materials and potentially impacting the field of spintronics. The cascade includes a primary density-wave instability, a nematic component, and an off-axis modulation with wave vectors geometrically tied to preceding orders, consistent with successive symmetry lowering in a Landau framework.

rss · arXiv Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics · Jul 14, 04:00

**Background**: Altermagnets are a class of magnetic materials with zero net magnetization but non-relativistic spin-split bands due to crystal symmetry. Unlike conventional antiferromagnets, their electronic bands are not Kramers degenerate, enabling unique spin-dependent properties. This study explores how such symmetry organizes collective electronic order, which was previously unexplored.

## [New Audit Protocol Validates ML Models for Materials Using DFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09910) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A model-agnostic audit protocol combining SHAP, counterfactual analysis, and Rashomon-style verification with DFT is proposed to validate both machine-learning models and their training data for materials properties, demonstrated on spin Hall conductivity. This addresses a critical gap in ML model validation in materials science by testing physical relevance of features and correctness of labels, improving trustworthiness of predictions. The method is cost-effective, requiring only a few DFT calculations, and can be applied to any composition space. The protocol uses SHAP attribution, counterfactual partial dependence analysis, and Rashomon-style cross-model verification, with findings adjudicated by targeted DFT. Applied to intrinsic spin Hall conductivity with a composition-only Random Forest, it achieves accuracy competitive with structure-aware graph networks while needing no relaxed crystal structure.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 14, 04:00

**Background**: Machine-learning models for materials properties often assume features reflect physics and training labels are correct, but standard validation does not test these assumptions. SHAP explains model predictions by feature contributions, counterfactual analysis explores how changes affect outcomes, and Rashomon-style verification checks consistency across different models. Density functional theory (DFT) is a first-principles method for computing materials properties from quantum mechanics. Spin Hall conductivity measures a material's efficiency in converting charge current to spin current, relevant for spintronics.

## [Anisotropic E(3)-Equivariant ML Coarse-Grained Potential](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10002) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The paper introduces a machine learning coarse-grained potential using equivariant message-passing neural networks with orientation-dependent features for anisotropic molecules, accurately reproducing structural and dynamical properties of liquid water. It demonstrates that including orientation information is essential for correct coarse-graining, and achieves 7-27x speedups with minimal computational overhead. This work addresses a key limitation in coarse-grained simulations by enabling accurate representation of anisotropic interactions, which is crucial for polar and asymmetric molecules. It provides a pathway to efficient mesoscopic simulations of complex molecular liquids, polymers, and biomolecular systems without sacrificing structural fidelity. The model represents atomic groups as massive ellipsoidal beads with orientation-dependent features, learning energies, forces, and torques directly from atomistic data. Comparison with an isotropic baseline shows systematic errors in short- and long-range order and degradation of angular correlations when orientation information is omitted.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 14, 04:00

**Background**: Coarse-graining reduces computational cost by grouping atoms into effective interaction sites, but often sacrifices structural fidelity or requires system-specific parameterization. Standard isotropic CG potentials lack orientation information, leading to inaccuracies for anisotropic molecules. Equivariant neural networks incorporate symmetry constraints like E(3) (Euclidean group) to improve learning of physical interactions.
