## [Flat Bands Observed in Bulk Type-II Weyl Semimetal TaRhTe4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01186) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers report the observation of flat bands near the Fermi level in bulk TaRhTe4, a noncentrosymmetric type-II Weyl semimetal, using angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES), contrary to density functional theory (DFT) predictions. This is significant because flat bands are rarely observed in Weyl semimetals, especially in nonmagnetic bulk systems, and their coexistence with nontrivial topology could enable new quantum phenomena and applications in topological electronics. The flat bands observed in TaRhTe4 were not predicted by DFT calculations, and the material is a van der Waals type-II Weyl semimetal with noncentrosymmetric structure, providing a rare platform for studying flat-band physics in a topological semimetal.

rss · arXiv Strongly Correlated Electrons · Jul 2, 04:00

**Background**: Flat bands are electronic bands with very little dispersion, leading to strong electron interactions and exotic states. Weyl semimetals host massless Weyl fermions and exhibit topological properties. Type-II Weyl semimetals have tilted Weyl cones, leading to unique transport phenomena. ARPES is a direct technique to measure electronic band structure in momentum space.

## [Facet-Selective Ballistic Supercurrent in Weak Topological Insulator ZrTe5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00683) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers report the observation of facet-selective ballistic supercurrent in Josephson junctions based on the weak topological insulator ZrTe5, with superconducting quantum interferometry revealing SQUID-like critical current oscillations tied to specific crystallographic facets. This work demonstrates a novel phenomenon—facet-selective supercurrent—and provides a platform for topological superconductivity and spatially controlled supercurrents, which could advance quantum computing and spintronics devices. The supercurrent is confined to two spatially separated side facets hosting gapless topological surface states, and the ballistic nature is evidenced by exponential temperature dependence and triangular interference lobes. The effect is robust across multiple devices and superconductors (Al and NbN).

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 2, 04:00

**Background**: Topological insulators have insulating bulk but conducting surface states. Weak topological insulators are a class where these surface states are restricted to specific facets. Josephson junctions consist of two superconductors separated by a weak link; here, the weak link is a weak topological insulator, allowing supercurrent to flow through topological surface states.

## [Direct spin-charge coupling enables multiferroic metals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22636) ⭐️ 9.0/10

This paper proposes a stacking engineering strategy to construct multiferroic metals from monolayer magnets, validated by first-principles calculations on six experimentally synthesized materials. These metals exhibit a linear magnetoelectric response originating from direct spin-charge interactions via field-modulated Fermi energy. This work introduces a novel mechanism for magnetoelectric coupling in metals, distinct from insulator-based mechanisms, offering potential for high-speed spintronic applications. It opens a new paradigm for designing functional multiferroic materials with strong magnetoelectric coupling. A universal formula for magnetoelectric coupling in these multiferroic metals is derived, rooted in direct spin-charge interactions. The strategy employs stacking engineering in van der Waals magnets and is validated on six materials, highlighting a fundamentally different mechanism from spin-orbit or spin-lattice coupling.

rss · arXiv Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics · Jul 2, 04:00

**Background**: Multiferroics are materials that simultaneously exhibit ferroelectricity and magnetism, with the magnetoelectric effect coupling electric polarization and magnetization. Most known multiferroics are insulators, as free carriers in metals tend to screen electric fields, making multiferroic metals rare. This work overcomes that by leveraging direct spin-charge interaction instead of spin-orbit or spin-lattice coupling mechanisms.

## [Non-Maxwellian Velocity Stats in Supercooled Liquids Linked to Viscosity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31985) ⭐️ 9.0/10

This paper presents numerical evidence that supercooled liquids can exhibit persistent non-Maxwellian velocity distributions, with excess kurtosis up to 0.3, which are connected to super-Arrhenius viscosity scaling. The finding challenges a fundamental assumption of equilibrium statistical mechanics, potentially reshaping the theoretical understanding of the glass transition and linking velocity statistics to transport anomalies. The authors introduced stochastic thermostats that do not enforce Maxwellian distributions, finding excess kurtosis κ between 0 and 0.3. Viscosity data collapse across 45 glass formers shows an average reduced temperature fluctuation width of 0.08, consistent with simulation kurtosis.

rss · arXiv Soft Condensed Matter · Jul 2, 04:00

**Background**: Equilibrium statistical mechanics predicts that particle velocities in a classical fluid at fixed temperature follow a Maxwellian distribution regardless of interactions. Supercooled liquids are metastable states that avoid crystallization and exhibit dramatically slowed dynamics, often described by super-Arrhenius viscosity, which has been difficult to explain from first principles.

## [Universal Scaling Theory of the Spin Hall Effect](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00343) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A new paper establishes a universal scaling theory for the spin Hall effect in Dirac electrons, identifying three distinct regimes and a novel dirty-regime scaling exponent of 0.6. This work unifies the spin Hall effect under a single scaling framework, analogous to the anomalous Hall effect, which could impact spintronics and condensed matter theory by resolving ambiguities in spin current definitions. The authors use the self-consistent T-matrix approximation to model short-range nonmagnetic impurities. They find that the spin Hall conductivity scales as ∝ σ^yy in the superclean regime, is constant in the moderately dirty regime, and ∝ (σ^yy)^0.6 in the dirty regime, which is distinct from the anomalous Hall effect exponent.

rss · arXiv Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics · Jul 2, 04:00

**Background**: The spin Hall effect (SHE) is a phenomenon where an electric current induces a transverse spin current due to spin-orbit coupling, even in non-magnetic materials. It is analogous to the anomalous Hall effect (AHE) in ferromagnets, for which a universal scaling behavior has been established. Understanding SHE scaling is important for spintronic devices. The self-consistent T-matrix approximation is a technique to treat impurity scattering beyond Born approximation, capturing multiple scattering effects.

This finding challenges the conventional understanding that only nontrivial bands can host extended states under disorder, and points to a previously overlooked topological manifestation that could impact how materials respond to impurities. The localizer index varies with energy within the trivial band's width, predicting nontrivial extended states as soon as disorder is introduced. This index provides a real-space topological invariant that captures structure beyond the standard Chern number.

**Background**: In condensed matter physics, the Chern number is a topological invariant that classifies band insulators. The study combines density functional theory and spin-dynamics simulations to show that the disordered skyrmion phase persists for external magnetic fields below 0.5 T. Kagome lattice magnets have garnered interest for their unique electronic bands, including Dirac cones and flat bands, which can host exotic quantum states. The topological Hall effect is a signature of non-zero scalar spin chirality, often associated with skyrmions.

The parting limit is the critical concentration of the reactive element required for continuous dealloying; in aqueous environments it is typically around 50–60 at.%. Molten salt environments have recently been shown to enable dealloying below this limit, but the mechanism was unclear.

## [Optimizing Nernst Thermoelectrics by Tuning Anomalous Hall and Nernst Angles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00401) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers demonstrated that the anomalous Nernst conductivity can be enhanced by regulating the sum of the anomalous Hall and Nernst angles to have the same sign, and validated this strategy experimentally in iron-doped Co3Sn2S2. This provides a new, generally applicable method to optimize Nernst thermoelectrics, addressing a key limitation in the field of topological thermoelectrics and potentially enabling more efficient energy conversion devices. The paper reveals a direct relation between the anomalous Nernst conductivity and the sum of the anomalous Nernst and Hall angles. It also reports a robust TlnT scaling law for the anomalous Nernst conductivity from 40 to 140 K in all studied samples, suggesting an intrinsic origin.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 2, 04:00

**Background**: The anomalous Nernst effect is a thermoelectric phenomenon in magnetic materials where a temperature gradient generates a transverse electric field, similar to the Hall effect but driven by heat. In Weyl semimetals, large anomalous Nernst effect has been observed, but methods to optimize it were limited. The anomalous Hall and Nernst angles measure the efficiency of converting charge current to transverse voltage or heat current, respectively. This work provides a strategy by tuning these angles.

## [BiP-PRISM accelerates core-loss STEM-EELS simulations on consumer GPUs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00756) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The BiP-PRISM algorithm introduces beam partitioning for both probe-forming and detector-propagating PRISM matrices, using natural-neighbor interpolation to reconstruct scattering matrices locally at ionized atoms, enabling full-resolution elemental mapping on consumer-grade GPUs. This reduces computational and memory costs dramatically, allowing researchers to perform high-fidelity core-loss EELS simulations without access to high-end computing clusters, broadening the accessibility of quantitative atomic-resolution elemental mapping. The algorithm achieves a 5x memory reduction in demonstrated examples like FePt nanoparticle Fe-L mapping, and validates that total error is governed solely by on-atom reconstruction error from the interpolation.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 2, 04:00

**Background**: Core-loss STEM-EELS simulations are computationally expensive because they require dynamical electron propagation before and after energy loss. The PRISM algorithm accelerates this by precomputing and reusing scattering matrices, but it still demands significant memory and computation. BiP-PRISM improves upon PRISM by partitioning beams and using natural-neighbor interpolation to reduce the number of beams that need to be explicitly calculated.
