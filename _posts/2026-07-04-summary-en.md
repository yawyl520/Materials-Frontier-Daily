## [Mixed-parity magnetism discovered in rare-earth tetraboride TbB4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02117) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers have uncovered a novel mixed-parity spin texture in the non-coplanar antiferromagnet TbB4, where in-plane spin components exhibit odd-parity p- and f-wave altermagnetism while the out-of-plane component shows even-parity d-wave altermagnetism, driven by scalar spin chirality rather than spin-orbit coupling. This work introduces a fundamentally new regime of mixed-parity spin splitting in compensated magnets, expanding the paradigm of altermagnetism and providing a platform for engineering complex spin-charge conversion phenomena with potential spintronic applications. Using first-principles calculations, tight-binding and k·p models, along with spin-group symmetry analysis, the study reveals that the staggered Berry phase from scalar spin chirality induces odd-parity in-plane textures, leading to distinct transport fingerprints such as non-relativistic Edelstein and spin Hall effects and a Berry curvature dipole.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 3, 04:00

**Background**: Altermagnetism is a recently recognized magnetic order in compensated collinear antiferromagnets that exhibits momentum-dependent spin splitting even without spin-orbit coupling, typically classified into even- or odd-parity textures. Scalar spin chirality characterizes the non-coplanar arrangement of spins and can give rise to topological phenomena via Berry phase effects.

## [Exact finite-size theory resolves conflation in chiral spin liquids](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01341) ⭐️ 9.0/10

This paper derives the exact parity-odd determinant of a gapped Dirac cone on a spatial cylinder, resummed to all orders in compact holonomy, fixing the precise map from spinon Chern number to physical spin Hall conductance. It resolves a long-standing confusion in chiral spin liquid theory by showing that finite-size corrections are strictly exponential, with no universal 1/L term, and provides a quantitatively verified bridge between microscopic topology and observable fractional response. The theory is validated on the kagome lattice via an exact parton band-structure calculation (C=-1, converging exponentially over cylinders 4-12 sites wide) and an interacting density-matrix renormalization group flux pump yielding ν_s = -0.500±0.011, matching analytic prediction without adjustable parameters.

rss · arXiv Strongly Correlated Electrons · Jul 3, 04:00

**Background**: Chiral spin liquids are topological phases that break time-reversal and parity symmetry, hosting fractionalized excitations called spinons and emergent gauge fields. The parity anomaly in (2+1)D quantum field theory implies that a gapped Dirac cone produces a half-integer Hall conductance, but the relationship between the Chern number of spinons, the level of Chern-Simons theory, and the physical spin Hall conductance has been conflated in the literature. This paper provides a finite-size theory that clarifies these connections.

## [Limits of energetic coupling between field dislocation mechanics and phase field crystal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01284) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A variational analysis reveals that the energetic coupling between Field Dislocation Mechanics (FDM) and the Phase Field Crystal (PFC) model only matches compatible (curl-free) parts of distortion fields, making it insensitive to the incompatible elastic distortion that encodes dislocation topology. This finding exposes a fundamental limitation in a previously proposed multiscale coupling approach, suggesting that the simple energetic coupling cannot effectively integrate dislocation mechanics with crystallography, which could hinder progress in multiscale modeling of plasticity. The coupling penalizes the L^2 distance between elastic distortion from FDM and configurational distortion from PFC, but acts as a divergence-driven forcing in the phase-field evolution that matches only compatible distortion. Numerical simulations show that this coupling cannot prevent unnatural core spreading in FDM.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 3, 04:00

**Background**: Field Dislocation Mechanics (FDM) is a continuum theory that solves initial boundary value problems for bodies with dislocation fields, while the Phase Field Crystal (PFC) model captures crystallographic structure at the atomic scale. The coupling proposed in 2020 aimed to reconcile dislocation mechanics with crystallography in a single framework by penalizing differences between elastic and configurational distortions.

## [Microscopic origin of inertial magnetization dynamics revealed](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01398) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new study using a non-Markovian quantum master equation shows that inertial magnetization dynamics in ferromagnets arise from coherent magnon-phonon coupling, explaining picosecond nutation and providing a microscopic basis for terahertz spin control. This work addresses a key open problem in ultrafast magnetism by identifying the microscopic mechanism of inertial dynamics, with potential to enable terahertz-frequency spintronic devices and new pathways for ultrafast magnetic switching. The researchers derived a non-Markovian quantum master equation that incorporates coherent interactions with optical phonons, showing that the fast optical phonon frequency explains the picosecond nutation. The model also accounts for variations between experiments through substrate-dependent phonon damping.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 3, 04:00

**Background**: Inertial magnetization dynamics refer to the nutation (a wobbling motion) of the magnetization vector occurring on picosecond timescales, beyond the usual precessional dynamics described by the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation. The origin of this inertial effect has been debated. A non-Markovian quantum master equation extends the standard Markovian (memoryless) treatment of open quantum systems to include memory effects. Magnon-phonon coupling is the interaction between spin waves (magnons) and lattice vibrations (phonons), which can hybridize to form magnon-polarons. This study proposes that coherent coupling to optical phonons is the microscopic source of the inertial term.

## [Low thermal conductivity in stoichiometric lithium niobate revealed](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01673) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers combined frequency-domain thermoreflectance (FDTR) measurements with machine-learned atomistic simulations to demonstrate that stoichiometric lithium niobate (sLN) exhibits intrinsically low thermal conductivity, over an order of magnitude lower than silicon. This finding is critical for thermal management in integrated photonics and electro-optic devices based on lithium niobate, as low thermal conductivity can lead to heat buildup and performance degradation. The study also provides a mechanistic understanding of phonon transport in sLN. The measured room-temperature thermal conductivity of sLN is orders of magnitude lower than that of classic semiconductors like silicon. The temperature-dependent conductivity follows a T⁻¹ scaling, indicating intrinsic phonon-phonon scattering dominance, and the maximum phonon mean free path is about 140 nm.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 3, 04:00

**Background**: Lithium niobate (LiNbO₃) is a widely used material for electro-optic and nonlinear optical devices, but its thermal transport properties have been insufficiently studied. Frequency-domain thermoreflectance (FDTR) is a non-contact laser-based technique that measures thermal properties of thin films by analyzing the phase lag of a reflected probe beam. Machine-learned atomistic simulations leverage neural networks to model interatomic potentials with near-first-principles accuracy, enabling large-scale simulations of phonon transport.
