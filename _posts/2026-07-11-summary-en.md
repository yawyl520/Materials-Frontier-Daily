## [Interfacial chirality enables field-free switching in vdW heterostructures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08023) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers demonstrated that interfacial chirality in all-van-der-Waals heterostructures of chiral Co1/3TaS2 and achiral Fe3GeTe2 or Fe3GaTe2 enables magnetic-field-free switching of perpendicular magnetization with ultralow current density (~10^6 A/cm²) and minimal power dissipation (<10^15 W/m³). This work introduces a new mechanism for energy-efficient spintronic devices, potentially enabling low-power magnetic memory and logic technologies without the need for external magnetic fields, which is a critical step toward practical spin-orbit torque applications. The heterostructures consist of a chiral van der Waals material Co1/3TaS2 and an achiral ferromagnet Fe3GeTe2 or Fe3GaTe2; the latter achieved room-temperature switching due to its higher Curie temperature. The switching is driven by two distinct unconventional spin-orbit torques originating from interfacial chirality.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 10, 04:00

**Background**: Van der Waals heterostructures are stacks of atomically thin layers held together by weak van der Waals forces, allowing precise control of interfaces. Chirality refers to structural asymmetry that lacks mirror symmetry, which can give rise to unique electronic and magnetic properties. Spin-orbit torque is a phenomenon where an electric current exerts torque on magnetization via spin-orbit coupling, enabling magnetization switching in spintronic devices.

## [Quantum weight and EELS signatures of Wannier quantum geometry in black phosphorus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08438) ⭐️ 9.0/10

This paper develops a first-principles DFT+Wannier framework to compute direction-resolved quantum weight in black phosphorus, showing that restricted quantum weight is measurable via low-loss EELS spectral moments. This work bridges quantum geometry theory with a practical bulk spectroscopic probe, enabling experimental access to integrated quantum metric via EELS in anisotropic materials. Using a 32-band DFT-Wannier Hamiltonian, the authors show that raw single-band quantum metric has singularities, necessitating masked maps and occupied-manifold projectors. The restricted in-plane quantum weight is nearly isotropic (K_zz/K_xx=0.972), despite strong band-mass anisotropy.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 10, 04:00

**Background**: Quantum geometry, including the quantum metric, describes the geometric properties of electronic wavefunctions in solids. The quantum weight, a related quantity, connects to the static structure factor and optical weight, and has been proposed as a fundamental property of quantum many-body systems. Low-loss EELS is a technique that probes collective excitations and can reveal integrated quantum geometric properties.

## [First-principles study overturns Dyakonov-Perel mechanism in MoS2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08479) ⭐️ 9.0/10

First-principles calculations reveal that intervalley scattering, not the Dyakonov-Perel mechanism, dominates exciton valley depolarization in monolayer MoS2, using a non-equilibrium Green's function approach. This challenges the established Dyakonov-Perel paradigm for valley relaxation in transition metal dichalcogenides, potentially impacting the design of valleytronic devices. The depolarization and decoherence time is approximately 50 fs at 300 K and 130 fs at 10 K, with intervalley scattering shortening depolarization by a factor of 3-4 compared to intravalley-only models.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 10, 04:00

**Background**: In monolayer transition metal dichalcogenides, valley polarization can be controlled via optical excitation. The Dyakonov-Perel mechanism attributes valley relaxation to motional narrowing from frequent intravalley scattering, which suppresses the effective magnetic field from valley-dependent exchange interaction. Intervalley scattering was considered secondary. The authors use a first-principles nonequilibrium Green's function approach with the generalized Kadanoff-Baym ansatz to treat exchange and scattering on equal footing.

First-principles calculations demonstrate that intercalating wide-band-gap insulating layers such as LaAlO₃, LaGaO₃, or LaScO₃ into Ruddlesden-Popper nickelates (La₂NiO₄ and La₃Ni₂O₇) provides a disorder-free route to electron doping, potentially achieving high-temperature superconductivity above 50 K. This work addresses the long-standing challenge of electron doping in nickelate superconductors, which has been elusive via conventional chemical substitution. The proposed heterostructuring method could open a new pathway to design high-temperature superconductors without introducing disorder, impacting the field of condensed matter physics and materials science. Hole doping has been achieved, but electron doping via cation substitution (e.g., substituting trivalent rare-earth with tetravalent ions) has failed due to thermodynamic instability. ## [Exceptionally high carrier mobility in hexagonal diamond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15076) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Ab initio calculations predict record-high electron and hole mobilities in hexagonal diamond (Lonsdaleite), reaching up to 28473 cm²/V·s for electrons and 6024 cm²/V·s for holes at room temperature, far exceeding most known semiconductors including cubic diamond. This discovery identifies hexagonal diamond as a potential next-generation wide-bandgap semiconductor for high-speed, high-power electronics, offering performance superior to traditional diamond. The identified suppression mechanisms for phonon scattering could guide the design of other high-mobility materials. The high mobility stems from two mechanisms: symmetry-enforced selection rules that suppress scattering by transverse acoustic phonons, and real-space electron-phonon decoupling due to mismatch between electronic wavefunctions and phonon-induced potentials. The effective masses are comparable to cubic diamond, so the mobility enhancement is not mass-related.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 10, 04:00

**Background**: Hexagonal diamond, also known as Lonsdaleite, is a carbon allotrope with a hexagonal crystal structure, distinct from the cubic structure of conventional diamond. It occurs naturally at meteorite impact sites. Carrier mobility measures how quickly charge carriers (electrons or holes) move in a material under an electric field; high mobility is crucial for fast electronic devices. Wide-bandgap semiconductors like diamond are valued for high-power and high-frequency applications.

## [DiffGLE: Learning Memory Kernels via Differentiable Simulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08424) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers have introduced DiffGLE, a method that uses differentiable simulation and a colored-noise ansatz to learn non-Markovian memory kernels for coarse-grained molecular dynamics, automatically enforcing fluctuation-dissipation consistency. This approach addresses a fundamental challenge in coarse-grained dynamics by enabling direct optimization of memory kernels from trajectory data, potentially improving the accuracy of dynamical properties in coarse-grained models for materials and soft matter. The method represents the random force by a trainable filter whose autocorrelation defines the friction memory, and is demonstrated on bulk water, carbon dioxide, and a star-polymer memory benchmark, accurately reproducing target velocity autocorrelation functions.

rss · arXiv Soft Condensed Matter · Jul 10, 04:00

**Background**: Coarse-grained molecular dynamics reduces computational cost by grouping atoms into beads, but capturing correct dynamics is challenging due to memory effects from eliminated degrees of freedom. The generalized Langevin equation (GLE) provides a theoretical framework with a memory kernel and colored noise, but parameterizing it is difficult. Differentiable simulation allows gradients of simulation outputs with respect to parameters to be computed via backpropagation, enabling optimization against reference data.

## [Monte Carlo Method Solves Kondo Model Exactly](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07797) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A new discrete model exactly reproduces the multichannel Kondo model and is simulated efficiently using cluster Monte Carlo algorithms, eliminating critical slowing down. This provides direct access to universal crossover functions and transport properties across all coupling regimes. This method unifies weak- and strong-coupling descriptions of the Kondo model, which were previously treated separately, and enables predictions for charge-Kondo devices. It represents a significant breakthrough in simulating quantum impurity problems with strongly correlated electrons. The method accommodates large channel numbers, anisotropy, interacting one-dimensional leads, and channel asymmetry. It uses cluster Monte Carlo algorithms to completely eliminate critical slowing down that plagues conventional simulations near phase transitions.

rss · arXiv Strongly Correlated Electrons · Jul 10, 04:00

**Background**: The Kondo model describes the interaction between a magnetic impurity and conduction electrons, leading to the Kondo effect where resistivity increases at low temperatures. The multichannel Kondo model generalizes this to multiple conduction electron channels, exhibiting exotic non-Fermi liquid behavior. Critical slowing down in Monte Carlo simulations refers to the dramatic increase in autocorrelation times near critical points, making it difficult to obtain accurate results. Cluster algorithms address this by updating large groups of variables collectively.

## [Bulk phase transition evidence in moiré rhombohedral graphene](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08710) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The paper reports bulk-sensitive measurements showing that the evolution from fractional Chern insulators to an anomalous Hall background in moiré rhombohedral graphene is due to a genuine bulk phase transition, not edge state equilibration. This work resolves a key controversy in the field and introduces mesoscopic probes as a powerful tool for studying competing ground states in moiré materials. Using a mesoscopic antidot device, the authors observed quasiparticles carrying one electron charge for both Chern states at fillings ν=1 and 2/3, and bulk resistance measurements indicate a thermodynamic phase transition below about 150 mK.

rss · arXiv Strongly Correlated Electrons · Jul 10, 04:00

**Background**: Moiré materials, such as twisted bilayer graphene, create flat bands that can host exotic electronic phases like fractional Chern insulators (FCIs). In moiré rhombohedral graphene (mRG), FCIs were observed to evolve into an anomalous Hall background at low temperatures, but whether this was due to edge state equilibration or a bulk phase transition remained unclear. The antidot device used here allows bulk-sensitive probing by creating a tunnel junction that avoids edge state contributions.

This work realizes a native flux-charge coupling, which was previously missing from the superconducting circuit toolbox.

## [Decoherence Induces Topological Phases in Lattice Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07801) ⭐️ 9.0/10

This paper demonstrates that decoherence, often considered destructive, can induce topological phases characterized by winding number and non-Hermitian skin effect in a lattice system with interaction-induced noise. This work overturns the conventional wisdom that decoherence always destroys topological order, opening a new route to engineer topological phases via correlated noise, with implications for quantum computation and non-Hermitian physics. The noise-averaged dynamics is governed by an interacting quantum master equation, yielding asymmetric diffusion with direction fixed by the winding number; the effect requires interactions and vanishes under postselection, confirming it as a genuine open-system phenomenon.

rss · arXiv Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics · Jul 10, 04:00

**Background**: Decoherence is the loss of quantum coherence due to environmental noise, typically viewed as detrimental for quantum phases. Topological phases are quantum states protected by global properties like winding numbers, while the non-Hermitian skin effect causes bulk states to localize at boundaries in non-conservative systems. This work shows that correlated noise can instead stabilize such topological behavior.
