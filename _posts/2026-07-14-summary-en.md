## [FAHCs Energetically Viable in Rhombohedral Graphene](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08822) ⭐️ 10.0/10

A new study demonstrates that fractional anomalous Hall crystals (FAHCs) are energetically competitive in rhombohedral pentalayer graphene using Monte Carlo simulations, bridging theory and experiment. This work establishes FAHCs as realizable states in a realistic material, advancing the search for topological order without external magnetic fields and guiding future experiments. The study used variational wavefunctions that are exact zero modes of an ideal model for R5G, then restored realistic dispersion and screened Coulomb interactions for energy evaluation. FAHCs are found to be competitive with integer anomalous Hall crystals and Fermi liquids.

rss · arXiv Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics · Jul 13, 04:00

**Background**: Fractional anomalous Hall crystals (FAHCs) are exotic states combining topological order from the fractional quantum Hall effect with spontaneous translation symmetry breaking, like a Wigner crystal. Rhombohedral pentalayer graphene (R5G) is a layered carbon structure exhibiting rich correlated and topological phases. Until now, FAHCs had only been theoretical speculation.

## [Electronic manipulation of polar order in electron crystal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09425) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers demonstrate that applying current pulses at room temperature can reversibly switch the charge-ordering direction in LuFe₂O₄, enabling non-reciprocal resistance memory operation. This breakthrough opens the door to information encoding directly in electron crystals, potentially leading to new types of memory devices that are faster and more energy-efficient than conventional ferroelectric memories. The non-reciprocal resistivity modulation disappears above the charge-ordering temperature (around 320 K), confirming the role of charge ordering. Numerical calculations incorporating inter-band Berry curvature reproduce the experimental observations.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 13, 04:00

**Background**: In strongly correlated materials, electrons can form ordered patterns known as charge-ordered states, similar to atomic crystals (Wigner crystals). These electron crystals can exhibit ferroelectric-like properties, and their ordering directions can theoretically encode information. Traditionally, such ordering has been manipulated by temperature or magnetic fields; electrical manipulation has been challenging.

SCGEN is a physics-grounded generative model that decouples crystallographic information generation from atomic coordinate optimization, using a variational autoencoder and a symmetry-constrained optimizer with universal machine-learning potentials to design stable, novel, and controllable crystal structures. SCGEN achieves state-of-the-art stability while preserving novelty, and it satisfies any specified composition, space group, lattice or joint constraint with 100% success without task-specific retraining, representing a significant advancement for inverse materials design. SCGEN addresses this by incorporating physical insights about crystallographic information (CI) and using symmetry-constrained optimization with universal machine learning potentials, which are pre-trained interatomic potentials that can predict energies and forces with near-DFT accuracy.

## [Quantum Geometric Friedel Oscillations Predicted in Flat-Band Metals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04654) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A new paper on arXiv predicts that quantum geometry, specifically the quantum metric, induces a distinct class of Friedel oscillations—termed quantum geometric Friedel oscillations (QGFOs)—in flat-band metals, with oscillation periods set by the momentum-space separation of quantum metric hot spots. Unlike conventional oscillations, QGFOs are robust against thermal smearing and can persist at temperatures much higher than the flat-band bandwidth. This work introduces a novel, experimentally detectable signature of quantum geometry in transport or scanning tunneling microscopy measurements, providing a direct probe of the quantum metric length and hot spot separations. It highlights the growing importance of quantum geometry beyond topological invariants, influencing phenomena like superconductivity and nonlinear transport. The QGFOs decay length is set by the quantum metric length, defined via integration of the quantum metric over the flat band, and is temperature-independent over a wide range, a manifestation of quantum metric protection. The period is determined by the momentum-space separation of quantum metric hot spots, which represent regions where the quantum metric is sharply peaked.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 13, 04:00

**Background**: Friedel oscillations are charge density ripples induced by an impurity in a metal, with a wavelength determined by the Fermi momentum. Quantum geometry, encoded in the quantum geometric tensor and its real part (the quantum metric), describes how Bloch wavefunctions change in momentum space and has been linked to phenomena like superfluid weight and nonlinear response. Flat-band metals—bands with very small bandwidth—are prime platforms for quantum geometric effects due to enhanced interactions and geometric contributions.

The theory combines the Doolittle equation for volume-dependent relaxation time with the Onsager variational principle to derive the constitutive relation, yielding an analytical expression for yield stress as a function of strain rate and temperature.

Traditional models often treat these phenomena separately. The Doolittle equation relates relaxation time to free volume, and the Onsager variational principle is a powerful tool for deriving dynamics from energy dissipation.

## [Screening-controlled dynamical criticality in the quantum Hall regime](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09244) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers combined temperature and current scaling to separately extract the dynamical exponent z and localization-length exponent γ at the quantum Hall plateau transition, using dual-graphite-gated graphene devices to tune the Coulomb interaction range. This work overcomes the previous limitation of measuring only a combined exponent, providing a direct way to probe how interactions affect spatial versus dynamical criticality. It establishes geometric screening as a versatile tool for controlling interactions in correlated two-dimensional systems. Temperature scaling gave κ ≈ 0.21 in the screened regime and κ ≈ 0.41 in the unscreened regime; combining with current scaling revealed z ≈ 1 (unscreened) to z ≈ 2 (screened), while γ remained near 2.4. The effective interaction range was tuned via the ratio of magnetic length to gate distance (l_B/d).

rss · arXiv Strongly Correlated Electrons · Jul 13, 04:00

**Background**: At continuous electronic phase transitions like the quantum Hall plateau transition, Coulomb interactions alter the relation between length, energy, and temperature, characterized by critical exponents. Previously, finite-temperature scaling alone could only measure the combined exponent κ = 1/(zγ), preventing separate determination of z and γ. Dual-graphite-gated graphene devices allow geometric tuning of the effective Coulomb interaction range by screening, enabling systematic study of interaction effects.

## [Majorana parity qubit demonstrated in coupled Kitaev chains](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09511) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers demonstrate coherent coupling between two-site Kitaev chains to realize a Majorana parity qubit, achieving coherent parity oscillations at the Majorana sweet spot. This work moves beyond identifying Majorana modes to coherent qubit control, a key milestone for topological quantum computing and fault-tolerant qubits. The qubit is encoded in the fermion parity of two coupled minimal Kitaev chains, with oscillation frequencies equal in both even and odd parity manifolds. Coherence depends systematically on inter-chain coupling and detunings, matching predictions for short, partially protected chains.

rss · arXiv Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics · Jul 13, 04:00

**Background**: Poor man's Majoranas are zero-energy states in two-site Kitaev chains, formed via balanced elastic cotunneling and crossed Andreev reflection. They have near-zero energy and equal electron-hole character but limited topological protection. A Majorana parity qubit uses the nonlocal fermion parity of two Majorana zero modes as the computational basis, offering protection against local noise.

It establishes the Kagome Hatsugai-Kohmoto model as a benchmark for interacting topological systems. At 1/3 filling, a weakly correlated Z2 topological insulator transitions to a strongly correlated insulator with spin Chern number Cs=2, and the transition shows critical scaling consistent with 2D Dirac fermions. At half-filling, the same model yields a non-Fermi-liquid to Mott-insulator transition.

**Background**: The Kagome lattice is a two-dimensional lattice of corner-sharing triangles, known for geometric frustration and nontrivial band topology. The Kane-Mele model describes a quantum spin Hall insulator with spin-orbit coupling, while the Hatsugai-Kohmoto interaction is an exactly solvable momentum-space interaction that enables analytical treatment of correlations.

## [Stochastic Similarity Renormalization Group via Quantum Monte Carlo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08830) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The paper introduces a stochastic similarity renormalization group framework (SRGQMC) that integrates quantum Monte Carlo to circumvent combinatorial complexity, achieving the first IMSRG(4) calculation. This breakthrough allows previously infeasible many-body calculations, particularly for nuclear interactions, by mitigating the combinatorial explosion in tensor-space flow equations. The method benchmarks well against deterministic free-space SRG and IMSRG(2)/(3) for realistic nucleon-nucleon interactions and the Richardson pairing model, and achieves the first IMSRG(4) calculation while deterministic extension remains unfeasible.

rss · arXiv Strongly Correlated Electrons · Jul 13, 04:00

**Background**: The similarity renormalization group (SRG) is a tool that transforms Hamiltonians to a band-diagonal form, but solving many-body flow equations becomes combinatorially complex at higher orders. Quantum Monte Carlo methods use random walkers to solve quantum many-body problems. By integrating these techniques, SRGQMC maps unitary transformations onto signed random walkers, avoiding the combinatorial tensor-space explosion.

## [Grain boundaries kinetically arrest graphite-to-diamond transformation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08868) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Large-scale molecular dynamics simulations reveal that grain boundaries in graphite promote local diamond nucleation but then arrest cooperative transformation propagation, leading to mixed sp2-sp3 nanocomposites under HPHT conditions. This finding identifies precursor microstructure as a missing control parameter in diamond synthesis, explaining the experimentally observed variability and offering a path to rationally design diamond-graphite nanocomposites. The simulations used realistic polycrystalline graphite models with grain sizes comparable to experiments; diamond growth propagates within individual grains but is arrested at crystallographically mismatched grain boundaries.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 13, 04:00

**Background**: The graphite-to-diamond transformation under high pressure and high temperature is a classic phase transition, but previous models focused on ideal single crystals, which cannot explain the frequent observation of mixed diamond-graphite intermediates. Grain boundaries are interfaces between crystals of different orientations, and their role in phase transformations has been studied in metals and ceramics, but not in carbon systems under HPHT.

## [Fast Homoepitaxy on (100) β-Ga2O3 Using Large Grown-In Offcuts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08929) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers achieved molecular beam epitaxy growth rates on (100) β-Ga2O3 substrates equal to the fast growth direction by using wafers with large grown-in offcuts (up to 13.4°), produced directly via edge-defined film-fed growth with rotated seed crystals. This work addresses a critical bottleneck in β-Ga2O3 epitaxy for power electronics, enabling scalable fabrication of (100)-oriented devices with high performance while avoiding material loss from conventional offcut methods. The offcut wafers were grown by EFG as 2D ribbons with rotated seeds, then polished for epitaxy; step-flow growth was observed across all offcut angles. The unintentional n-type doping was 2×10^15 cm^-3, one of the lowest for MBE-grown β-Ga2O3, and planar Schottky barrier diodes achieved an on/off ratio ~10^5 and average breakdown field 1.56 MV/cm.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 13, 04:00

**Background**: β-Ga2O3 is an ultrawide-bandgap semiconductor promising for high-power electronics, but its low symmetry causes anisotropic growth rates. The (100) orientation, desirable for scalable wafers, typically suffers 10-30x slower growth rates. Offcut substrates can enhance growth, but conventional methods waste material; this work uses large grown-in offcuts formed during crystal growth to solve that issue.

## [ML accelerates DFTB by predicting initial charges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09304) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The authors introduce a machine learning model that uses SOAP descriptors and kernel ridge regression to predict initial atomic charges for DFTB calculations, significantly reducing the number of self-consistent iterations needed for convergence. This approach directly tackles a major bottleneck in DFTB simulations, enabling faster large-scale and high-throughput materials screening. It demonstrates a practical synergy between machine learning and semiempirical methods, potentially broadening the applicability of DFTB to more complex systems. The model is element-specific and trained on reference DFTB calculations using the Smooth Overlap of Atomic Positions (SOAP) descriptor and kernel ridge regression. It was validated on diverse systems including organic molecules, biomolecules, water clusters, transition metal oxides, and solid electrolytes.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 13, 04:00

**Background**: Density-Functional Tight-Binding (DFTB) is a semiempirical electronic structure method that balances accuracy and computational cost. Its self-consistent charge (SCC) scheme often requires many iterations to converge, especially for complex systems. The Smooth Overlap of Atomic Positions (SOAP) descriptor provides a rotationally invariant representation of local atomic environments, and kernel ridge regression is a machine learning technique that can map these descriptors to target properties. Predicting good initial charges can warm-start the SCC loop, reducing iterations.

This model addresses a key challenge in alloy design by enabling preliminary evaluation of kinetic phase competition, which could accelerate the discovery of new alloys with tailored microstructures. The model includes a topology-dependent viscosity correction with a smoothed BCC multiplier and a continuous correction factor based on mixing enthalpy, excess entropy, and atomic-size dispersion. It captures changes in lattice type expected from valence electron concentration and distinguishes alloys with high and low glass-forming ability.

Phase selection—whether the solid is crystalline and which crystal structure forms—is critical for material properties. Multicomponent concentrated alloys contain several principal elements, making phase prediction more complex. Glass-forming ability (GFA) describes how easily an alloy can avoid crystallization during cooling.
