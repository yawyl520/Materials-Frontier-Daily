## [Atomic Chirality Engineering Boosts Piezoelectricity in Selenium Nanowires](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03063) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers propose atomic chirality engineering as a new design principle for piezoelectricity, demonstrating that crystal handedness alone controls performance in trigonal selenium nanowires, with right-handed D-Se wires achieving higher effective piezoelectric coefficients than left-handed ones. This work introduces a new degree of freedom—atomic chirality—for enhancing piezoelectric materials without altering composition, enabling more efficient energy harvesting from weak biomechanical sources for wearable electronics and IoT devices. Using piezoresponse force microscopy, the team resolved consistent enantiomeric differences, and flexible nanogenerators and acoustic sensors built from the two enantiomers produced distinct outputs under identical deformation, confirming chirality as the sole cause of performance variation.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 7, 04:00

**Background**: Piezoelectric materials generate electric charge when mechanically deformed; conventional enhancement strategies include composition optimization, defect engineering, and strain engineering. Chirality at the atomic scale is a less-explored parameter that can influence dipole alignment in non-centrosymmetric crystals. Trigonal selenium naturally possesses a chiral crystal structure with helical atomic chains, making it an ideal model system to isolate the effect of lattice handedness on piezoelectricity.

Researchers introduced Dyna-Mat-v1.0, a benchmark dataset of first-principles molecular dynamics trajectories for evaluating foundation machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) under realistic finite-temperature conditions. They evaluated 15 foundation MLIPs across four model tiers on structural and dynamical observables. This benchmark addresses a critical gap by providing ground-truth data for finite-temperature validation of foundation MLIPs, which are increasingly used as drop-in replacements for first-principles calculations. The findings highlight limitations in pressure description and show that low force errors do not guarantee accurate structural predictions, guiding future model development. The benchmark includes condensed-phase first-principles MD trajectories and compares single-point energy/force errors with observables from MLIP-driven trajectories. Pressure remains poorly described across most models, and some systems with low force errors produce qualitative failures in predicted structure.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 7, 04:00

**Background**: Foundation machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) are models trained on large datasets of first-principles calculations to act as fast surrogates for density functional theory (DFT). Finite-temperature molecular dynamics simulations require accurate description of atomic forces at non-zero temperatures, but most validation of MLIPs has been limited to static or harmonic benchmarks. Dyna-Mat provides trajectory-based reference data to test MLIPs under realistic conditions.

## [New method disentangles electronic and lattice effects in perovskite transient absorption](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04840) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers developed a first-principles approach combining nonequilibrium Bethe-Salpeter equation with ab initio molecular dynamics to separate electronic and lattice contributions in transient absorption spectra of CH3NH3PbBr3. They found that electronic effects dominate on femtosecond timescales, while thermal lattice effects become significant on picosecond timescales. This work addresses a key challenge in interpreting time-resolved spectroscopy of metal halide perovskites, enabling quantitative analysis of transient absorption across femtosecond-to-picosecond timescales. It provides crucial insights into the photophysics of soft semiconductors, which are important for applications in solar cells and light-emitting devices. In CH3NH3PbBr3, on femtosecond scales, photoinduced Coulomb screening blueshifts excitonic resonances while Pauli blocking is negligible; on picosecond scales, lattice vibrations redistribute X-ray absorption, while lattice expansion blueshifts optical absorption. The method quantitatively disentangles electronic and thermal lattice contributions for the first time.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 7, 04:00

**Background**: Transient absorption spectroscopy is a powerful pump-probe technique to study ultrafast dynamics in materials, but in metal halide perovskites, strong electron-phonon coupling makes it difficult to separate electronic and lattice contributions. The Bethe-Salpeter equation is a many-body method for calculating excitonic effects, and its nonequilibrium extension allows modeling of time-resolved spectra. This study combines it with ab initio molecular dynamics to capture thermal lattice effects.

## [New torsional selection rule for spin-orbit conversion of light](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05142) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers derived a new selection rule Δℓ = q for spin-orbit conversion in torsional media, distinct from the standard Pancharatnam-Berry rule Δℓ = 2q, enabling generation of topologically quantized orbital angular momentum via medium torsion. This work fundamentally extends the spin-orbit conversion framework beyond the conventional Pancharatnam-Berry phase, opening new avenues for structured light generation and angular momentum manipulation in photonics. The rule arises from the contortion tensor, a rank-one vector field, and simulations show 83% conversion efficiency over three Rayleigh ranges. The conserved screw charge  J̃_z = L_z + (q/2)σ_z and exchange of (2−q)ħ angular momentum per photon with the defect lattice are key features.

**Background**: Spin-orbit conversion of light typically uses anisotropic media with a director field, obeying the Pancharatnam-Berry selection rule Δℓ = 2q, where q is the texture charge. This new work introduces geometric torsion as a distinct mechanism, where the medium's screw-dislocation array (continuum torsion) mediates conversion via a vector field, fundamentally changing the allowed OAM output.

The study used real-time TDDFT with explicit treatment of all electrons for beryllium self-irradiation, revealing that electron capture by the projectile core from host cores is behind the suppression of valence excitations, rather than Pauli blocking as previously proposed.

**Background**: Electronic stopping power quantifies the energy loss of a swift ion moving through matter due to electronic excitations. ## [Nonreciprocal coupling creates a new universality class](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02661) ⭐️ 9.0/10

This paper shows that nonreciprocal coupling between an order parameter and a conserved diffusive density leads to a novel non-equilibrium universality class, with violation of fluctuation-dissipation relations as a measurable hallmark. This finding challenges the exceptional robustness of the Ising universality class under non-equilibrium conditions, providing a new framework for understanding critical phenomena in driven systems and offering a clear experimental signature. Using a two-loop renormalization group calculation, the authors find a correction-to-scaling exponent ω ≈ 0.020 in three dimensions, indicating strong finite-size corrections, and show that Ising universality is recovered only at d=2.

rss · arXiv Soft Condensed Matter · Jul 7, 04:00

**Background**: The Ising universality class describes the critical behavior of a wide range of equilibrium phase transitions and is known to be robust to many non-equilibrium perturbations. Nonreciprocal coupling means that the interaction between two fields is not symmetric, breaking detailed balance. This paper introduces a conserved diffusive density that couples nonreciprocally to an order parameter, leading to a fast-diffusion fixed point where the density acts as long-range multiplicative noise.

## [New non-equilibrium universality class in Brownian Ising Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02667) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A complete field-theoretical renormalization-group analysis of the Brownian Ising Model reveals a new non-equilibrium fixed point with distinct critical exponents, including a negative anomalous dimension η, which is absent in equilibrium Ising universality. This discovery establishes the Brownian Ising Model fixed point as the unique infrared attractor for any nonzero diffusion constant in three dimensions, fundamentally altering our understanding of non-equilibrium phase transitions and providing a testable signature of fluctuation-dissipation theorem violation. Using the Martin-Siggia-Rose formalism and an epsilon=4-d expansion, the authors show that coupling between the Z2 order parameter and a conserved density drives the system to a new fixed point, with correlation and response functions acquiring different anomalous dimensions (η ≠ 2 - γ/ν), a direct signature of detailed balance breaking. An exact scaling relation ν = 2/(d+z-2) is derived from symmetry arguments.

rss · arXiv Soft Condensed Matter · Jul 7, 04:00

**Background**: The Ising model is a classic lattice model of ferromagnetism in statistical mechanics. Non-equilibrium phase transitions occur in systems driven away from thermal equilibrium, often breaking detailed balance. The Brownian Ising Model extends the Ising model by coupling the spin order parameter to a diffusing conserved density, leading to non-equilibrium steady states. This paper applies field theory and renormalization group methods to classify the universal behavior.
