## [Co-Doped Fe5GeTe2 Monolayers Show Ferromagnetism up to 200 K](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05962) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers synthesized epitaxial monolayers of Co-doped Fe5GeTe2 using molecular beam epitaxy, achieving ferromagnetic ordering up to ~200 K in monolayers and above room temperature in multilayers. This work pushes the Curie temperature of van der Waals monolayers beyond the typical 150 K limit, enabling potential applications in spintronics and 2D magnetic devices. The study combines X-ray magnetic circular dichroism and density functional theory to identify Co substitution sites and explain the enhanced intralayer exchange interactions. The magnetic properties are primarily governed by intralayer interactions, as trends in Curie temperature and anisotropy are similar across thicknesses.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 8, 04:00

**Background**: Fe5GeTe2 is a van der Waals material with ferromagnetism near room temperature in bulk. However, isolating monolayers with high Curie temperature has been challenging. Molecular beam epitaxy allows precise control over thickness and composition. XMCD is an element-specific technique to probe magnetic moments.

## [Cr-Doped FeSb2 Exhibits Altermagnetic Order Below 3.5 K](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01088) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Experimental evidence shows that Cr-doped FeSb2 exhibits altermagnetic order below 3.5 K, breaking time-reversal symmetry without net magnetization, as confirmed by magnetization, transport, and muon spin relaxation measurements. This work provides compelling experimental evidence for altermagnetic order in a candidate material, advancing the understanding of altermagnets which could have applications in spintronics. Magnetization measurements indicate spin-compensated ordering below ~3.5 K with moments along the b-axis, while muon spin relaxation confirms bulk magnetic ordering, but neutron diffraction shows no long-range collinear order, suggesting short-range or disordered state.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 8, 04:00

**Background**: Altermagnets are a newly recognized class of magnetic materials with compensated magnetic moments and spin-split electronic bands without net magnetization, unlike antiferromagnets. They combine advantages of ferromagnets and antiferromagnets, offering potential for spintronics. Cr-doped FeSb2 was theoretically proposed as a d-wave altermagnet, but its magnetic ground state was unresolved.

## [Doubly chiral active particles enable robust topological edge transport](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06193) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers introduced doubly chiral active Brownian particles (dcABPs) that combine intrinsic angular velocity with translation-rotation coupling, demonstrating topologically protected transport along boundaries without backscattering at corners via theory, simulation, and experiment. This work bridges active matter and topological physics, offering a new platform for robust transport in soft matter systems that is insensitive to defects and corners, with potential applications in microfluidics and autonomous robotic swarms. The double chirality arises from two mechanisms: intrinsic rotation independent of translation, and a translation-rotation coupling from asymmetric friction distribution perpendicular to self-propulsion. Topological protection emerges only when the two chiralities have opposite signs and the intrinsic rotation is weaker than the coupling.

**Background**: Active Brownian particles (ABPs) are self-propelled colloids that convert energy into motion, often exhibiting chiral (circular) motion due to intrinsic rotation. Topological protection refers to the immunity of edge modes to backscattering, commonly studied in quantum systems but rare in classical active matter. Previous chiral ABPs showed edge currents but lacked topological protection as they backscatter at corners.

## [Cavity-Enhanced FQHE Gaps in Composite Fermion Theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06298) ⭐️ 9.0/10

This theoretical study shows that cavity-mediated attractive electron-electron interactions enhance both the transport gap and magnetoroton spectrum of fractional quantum Hall states at filling factors ν=1/3 and 1/5, computed within the composite-fermion framework. It bridges cavity quantum electrodynamics with fractional quantum Hall physics, offering a potential route to engineer stronger correlations and more robust topological states in quantum materials. The transport gap enhancement scales quadratically with electron number and with the fourth power of the vacuum-field gradient, while the magnetoroton gap shows a richer dependence on the interaction range, with the high-k magnetoroton gap enhanced for all ranges.

rss · arXiv Mesoscale and Nanoscale Physics · Jul 8, 04:00

**Background**: The fractional quantum Hall effect (FQHE) occurs in two-dimensional electron gases under strong magnetic fields, where interactions lead to topologically ordered states. Composite fermions are quasiparticles formed by binding electrons to an even number of magnetic flux quanta, simplifying the FQHE as an integer quantum Hall effect of composite fermions. Magnetorotons are neutral collective excitations of FQHE states, analogous to rotons in superfluid helium. Cavity-mediated interactions arise from coupling electronic degrees of freedom to quantized cavity photon modes, introducing nonlocal electron-electron interactions.

This discovery establishes synthetic quantum matter as a powerful platform for probing chiral graviton modes, which are key signatures of quantum geometry in fractional quantum Hall states, and could enable experimental studies in cold-atom systems. **Background**: Fractional quantum Hall (FQH) states are topological phases that emerge in two-dimensional electron systems under strong magnetic fields, featuring fractionally charged excitations. Chiral graviton modes are neutral spin-2 collective excitations that serve as quanta of quantum metric fluctuations, recently observed in conventional FQH liquids. The Harper-Hofstadter model provides a lattice realization of FQH physics in ultracold atomic gases.

## [Topological Insulators Exhibit Negative Surface Stiffness Causing Chaos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21624) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers discovered that topological insulators possess intrinsic negative surface stiffness, a property derived from their electronic boundary states, which drives spatiotemporal chaos during interface growth. This instability is fundamentally different from conventional crystal growth instabilities driven by external non-uniformities. This finding reveals a new intrinsic mechanism for interfacial instability in topological materials, potentially altering how we understand and control the growth of these important quantum materials. It could lead to improved synthesis methods and open new avenues for studying nonlinear dynamics in material systems. The interface dynamics is governed by the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation, a prototypical nonlinear equation exhibiting spatiotemporal chaos. While trivial insulators have positive stiffness that smooths surfaces, topological insulators exhibit negative stiffness that amplifies small shape fluctuations.

**Background**: Topological insulators are materials that are insulating in their interior but conduct electricity on their surfaces due to topologically protected surface states. In conventional crystal growth, interfacial instabilities are typically driven by external factors like diffusion or thermal gradients. Surface stiffness is a measure of how strongly a surface resists changes in its shape; positive stiffness stabilizes flat surfaces, while negative stiffness amplifies perturbations.

## [New Braiding Statistics in p+q+1 Dimensions Redefines Three-Loop Braiding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06279) ⭐️ 9.0/10

This paper identifies a novel braiding statistical process W_N(X,Y) for order-N excitations in p+q+1 spatial dimensions, detecting the Bockstein response A∪β(B). It shows that conventional three-loop braiding requires non-Abelian fusion rules and thus falls outside the new classification for Abelian fusion rules. This work challenges the conventional understanding of braiding statistics in topological phases and provides a new classification framework. It has significant implications for the study of topological order, anyons, and quantum matter by redefining the relationship between statistics and symmetry anomalies. The new statistical process is defined as W_N(X,Y) = (Y^{-1}X^{-1})^N (Y X)^N for two order-N excitations. For loop excitations with Abelian fusion rules forming a group G, all such statistics are classified by H^5(B^2G, U(1)), but conventional three-loop braiding goes beyond this classification, implying it necessarily has non-Abelian fusion rules.

rss · arXiv Strongly Correlated Electrons · Jul 8, 04:00

**Background**: Braiding statistics describe the phase factors acquired when exchanging topological excitations in quantum matter. Conventionally, braiding of p- and q-dimensional excitations is defined in p+q+2 spatial dimensions. The Bockstein homomorphism is a cohomology operation used in algebraic topology to detect torsion. Three-loop braiding involves braiding one loop around another while both are linked to a third loop, and has been studied in (Z_N)^K gauge theories. This paper re-examines these concepts in one lower dimension and reveals new possibilities.

## [Anomalously high thermal conductivity in Hg1201 hints at multiple Fermi surface sheets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06332) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers measured the thermal conductivity of underdoped HgBa2CuO4+δ (Tc=76 K, p=0.11) at dilution refrigerator temperatures and extracted a nodal quasiparticle velocity ratio v_F/v_Δ = 23±3, anomalously high compared to other cuprates. Combined with previous specific heat data, this suggests multiple Fermi surface sheets crossing the nodal line. This result challenges the conventional single-Fermi-surface picture of cuprate superconductivity and points to a more complex electronic structure that could revise our understanding of the pseudogap phase and pairing mechanism. It may impact theories of high-Tc superconductivity, especially in the underdoped regime. The extraction of v_F/v_Δ from thermal conductivity assumes a single line node per quadrant, yet the obtained value of 23±3 is anomalously large relative to other cuprates. Together with previously reported high normal-state specific heat, this suggests more than one Fermi surface sheet, possibly including a small electron pocket beyond the one detected by quantum oscillations.

rss · arXiv Strongly Correlated Electrons · Jul 8, 04:00

**Background**: Cuprate superconductors, such as HgBa2CuO4+δ (Hg1201), exhibit high-temperature superconductivity when doped with holes. Their electronic structure features a d-wave superconducting gap with nodes (points where the gap vanishes) along certain directions. The nodal quasiparticle velocity ratio v_F/v_Δ characterizes the Fermi velocity relative to the gap slope, and its value can reveal details of the Fermi surface. In underdoped cuprates, the Fermi surface is known to be reconstructed into small pockets, but the exact topology is debated. This study uses low-temperature thermal conductivity, a bulk probe, to investigate the nodal quasiparticle behavior.

DeepH-E3 is an E(3)-equivariant neural network that can predict the DFT Hamiltonian from atomic structures, enabling efficient calculations. Shift current is a nonlinear optical response that produces a dc photocurrent in noncentrosymmetric materials, relevant for photovoltaic applications.

## [Hydrogen diffusion model validated in irradiated bcc metals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06122) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Molecular dynamics simulations validated an analytic model for hydrogen effective diffusivity in multi-occupancy traps in irradiated tungsten and vanadium, achieving good agreement without any fitted parameters. This validation enables first-principles predictive simulations of hydrogen isotope diffusion and retention in irradiated microstructures, crucial for assessing material performance and tritium inventory in nuclear fusion reactors. The study used molecular dynamics to compute effective diffusivity in monovacancies in W and V, and voids in W, showing good agreement with the analytic multi-occupancy trap model of Kaur et al. (2025, Phys. Rev. Mater. 9:125404) using only physically motivated parameters from DFT or empirical potentials.

rss · arXiv Materials Science · Jul 8, 04:00

**Background**: In nuclear fusion reactors, plasma-facing materials such as tungsten are exposed to high neutron fluxes, creating nanoscale defects like vacancies and voids that trap hydrogen isotopes. Traditional diffusion models assume each trap can hold only one hydrogen atom, but experiments show that defects can accommodate multiple hydrogen atoms (multi-occupancy). This study validates an analytic model that captures multi-occupancy effects, enabling more accurate predictions of hydrogen retention and transport in radiation-damaged metals.
